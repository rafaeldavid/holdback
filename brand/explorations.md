# Mascot explorations: mule, donkey, crow

Draft, 2026-10-31. Rafa asked to explore a donkey, and a crow tied to the word escrow ("yscrow"). The mule (`mascot.md`) is the current pick; this file compares the three.

## At a glance

| | Mule | Donkey | Crow |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Core idea | Carries the load | Guards the herd | Holds the cache, remembers, judges |
| Fit with the hold | Strong: carries the sealed crate | Medium: carries, and guards | Strong: crows cache food and come back for it |

## Donkey

**Why it could work.** Donkeys carry loads like mules, and farmers keep them as livestock guardians: a donkey will confront a fox or a dog to protect sheep and goats. That gives a protective story ("it guards what's held") that the mule lacks.

**Why it's weaker.**
- "Ass" and "jackass" invite jokes at the worst moments (a refund, a dispute).
- In the US the donkey is the Democratic Party's symbol; a neutral protocol shouldn't wear a party's mascot.
- Popular culture's best-known donkey is a comic sidekick.

**Verdict:** keep the guardian idea, not the animal. The mule already carries the load; "guards the hold" can be part of the mule's story.

## Crow ("escrow" → "crow")

**Why it could work.** The animal is already inside the word: es**crow**. And crows match the product on four counts:

1. **Caching.** Crows and other corvids hide food and come back for it later: a hold, kept safe until the right moment.
2. **Memory.** Crows recognise individual human faces and remember who wronged them for years (University of Washington field studies). That matches D26: both sides remember the exchange.
3. **Judgment.** Folklore across Britain and Europe tells of "crow courts" where a flock gathers to judge one of its own. Real crows do gather around a dead crow, which researchers read as learning about danger. A gathering that decides is close to an adjudicator panel. (The court is folklore; say so.)
4. **Intelligence.** Corvids use tools and solve multi-step puzzles, which suits a product for AI agents.

**Name and wordmark ideas** (none checked for trademarks yet):
- **esCROW** as a wordmark: lowercase "es", then "crow" set heavier, the crow perched on the last letter.
- A **crow** mark for Holdback: keep the name, use the crow as the emblem.
- **Yscrow**: Rafa's spelling. Distinctive and easy to search, but readers won't know how to say it; test "ees-crow" against "why-scrow" aloud before using it.
- **Caw** for the mascot's name; **Cache** for a feature name (the hold).

**Risks to manage.**
- **"Thief of shiny things."** The worst trait for something that holds other people's money. Draw the crow *guarding* a sealed crate, never carrying off a coin or a ring.
- **Omen of death; "a murder of crows".** Keep the style warm: a rounded silhouette, a bright eye, daylight colours, no gothic black-on-black.
- **"Eat crow".** Minor; it only bites in copy about losing a dispute.
- **Busy field.** Corvids are common in tech and crypto branding; a trademark and app-store search comes first.

## Recommendation

- **Crow**, if we want the name and the mascot to reinforce each other. It carries the most meaning (hold, memory, judgment, intelligence), and its main risk, theft, can be designed against.
- **Mule**, if we want plain, dependable infrastructure and no clever wordplay. Its main risk ("money mule") is harder to design away, because it's a term compliance teams use daily.
- **Donkey**: no. Borrow its guardian story for whichever animal wins.

Next steps before choosing: a trademark and domain search for the crow names, and a quick read by one compliance person and one AI-lab researcher of both marks side by side.
