## Intent

Every timeout on HoldbackRules is permissionless: once its deadline passes, anyone may send it, and the contract checks it against `dealOf` and the terms. Today the operators' pass sends only the fallback takeover, at `judgeBy`, for their own fallback adjudicator. The party timeouts (the reclaim, the default and the offer expiry) wait for a caller. Since T-228 that caller can be anyone with no gas, through `POST /v1/commitments/<id>/triggers/<kind>`. But someone still has to come back, so a deal whose parties went away keeps its money held.

This task makes the operators' pass send each due timeout of every deal the world tracks, at the relayer's gas, through the same relay path the trigger route uses (`EvmSettlement.trigger`). The pass waits one minute past each deadline first, so a party who is there sends its own and the two do not race (Decisions). That includes a deal behind the chain (residual 13), whose book stopped: the pass reads `dealOf` for it instead. The pass reads whether a timeout is due at a pinned block, never before a block this node already applied (`docs/team.md`, a chain read after a transaction). It sends each timeout once, across restarts, and it respects T-219's lane rows. Telemetry counts each timeout the operators sent. The relayer's gas is guarded: only deals this world holds, only calls the contract accepts at the pinned block, a cap per pass, a daily budget, and a back-off per deal after any refusal.

A party may still trigger a timeout itself, the moment it is due. Because the operators may now have sent it first, a trigger whose timeout already ran answers 200 with the state that timeout left, and sends nothing. Round 1's driver sends its own triggers (`examples/e2e-run.ts`, `trigger`), and before this change such a late trigger answered 409 `invalid_state` and failed the scenario.

**Non-goals.**
- The fallback takeover before `fallbackBy`. It is the fallback adjudicator's own turn, and the reference adjudicator declines one it would not decide (T-211). The pass sends the takeover only at `fallbackBy`, when the default needs it.
- Offers no adjudicator accepted. Nobody can fund them, and posting one is free.
- SimChain worlds, the operators' adjudicators acting on a deal behind the chain (T-227), signed steps, contract changes and schema changes.
- Pinning `send`'s own simulation to the deal's last block. That belongs to T-232, the read-after-write audit (D54), which starts once this PR and T-230's merge.

## Acceptance criteria

1. [x] **Each timeout closes a deal with no caller.** On anvil, for each one, a deal reaches its due time and the pass sends it; no party sends a transaction or a trigger. Tests in `packages/settlement/evm/timeouts.test.ts`: the reclaim, the default after an unheard escalation, the takeover and default for a silent primary and fallback, an accepted offer's expiry, an unused appeal window, and an appeal nobody decides (the appellant gets its bond back).
2. [x] **A timeout that is not due is not sent.** Before each deadline, and within the minute's grace after it, the pass makes no chain read and sends nothing; the takeover before `fallbackBy` is not sent; an offer no adjudicator accepted gets no transaction.
3. [x] **The contract is the check, at a pinned block.** A timeout the book thinks due but the contract refuses at the pinned block is not sent, and the next pass does not read it again (back-off). The block is the latest head, the sync's cursor or the highest block in the log, whichever is latest; a test with an RPC head that trails every write shows it.
4. [x] **Once, across a restart and a lost receipt read.** A restart between the broadcast and the receipt sends the reclaim once: the restarted pass leaves the deal to the lane row's recovery, which lands it, and then has nothing to send. A reclaim mined while every read of its block fails is not sent again: its lane row stays open, and the pass leaves the deal to the sync. Each send carries an Idempotency-Key the world derives from its secret.
5. [x] **A deal behind the chain still closes.** T-228's case (the funding and the delivery in one Multicall3 transaction): the pass reads `dealOf`, sends the takeover and then the default at `fallbackBy`, and the buyer gets the hold back. It closes too after a restart whose recovery read lagged.
6. [x] **Gas guards.** A cap per pass (4 sends, 16 reads), money first; a daily budget of 200 timeouts, offer expiries capped at 50 of them, in one storage row a UTC day written before each send and given back when nothing went out; a back-off per deal after each refusal or failure; a pause for every deal when the relay refuses (out of test ETH, above the gas cap).
7. [x] **A party's late trigger is not an error.** After the operators' timeout, the same trigger answers 200 with the state it left, with no event and no transaction.
8. [x] **In the world.** The world's pass runs it after the sync and the adjudicators, sets the alarm at the next timeout, and counts each send in telemetry (`operatorTimeouts`, by kind). Test: `apps/demo-cloud/src/timeouts.anvil.test.ts`.
9. [x] **Docs.** `docs/api.md` and `site/llms.txt` say deals close on their own a minute or two after a deadline, a party may still trigger one the moment it is due, and when a late trigger still answers 409; `docs/neutrality.md` (the operator's row and residual 13); T-228's leftover marked done; this row in `docs/plan.md`.
10. [x] `pnpm verify` green; review and red-team findings fixed or recorded (Evidence, Decisions).

## Out of scope

- A daily budget for the trigger route itself. Since T-228 any client can ask the operators to send a due timeout, bounded only by the write throttle (120 writes a minute per client). T-231's budget counts only the timeouts the pass sends.
- Sending the timeouts from several lanes at once: the pass sends them one after another, each waiting for its block.

## Decisions

- 2026-11-06: **what the pass sends.** Every due timeout of a deal the world holds, except two. The takeover before `fallbackBy` stays the fallback adjudicator's own turn (T-211 lets it decline one). An offer no adjudicator accepted gets nothing, since nobody can fund it and posting one is free. A silent fallback is therefore taken over at `fallbackBy`, and the default follows on the next pass. The outcome is the same as a takeover at `judgeBy` followed by silence: the contract refunds the buyer at `fallbackBy` either way.
- 2026-11-06: **appeal expiries for every decider.** The demo adjudicators already expire the appeals of their own decisions (T-211), and the pass runs after them, so it finds those closed. Two cases still needed the pass. An open appeal that nobody decides holds the appellant's bond until someone expires it. An outside decider's unused window holds that decider's bond. The pass sends both. The first is rank 0 (money waits on it), the second rank 1.
- 2026-11-06: **the check reads at a pinned block.** The block is the latest head the RPC reports, raised to the sync's cursor and to the highest block of any transaction in this node's log. A load-balanced node's head can trail a write, so a check never reads before a block this node already applied (`docs/team.md`). The contract answers there with an `eth_call` of the timeout itself. For a deal behind the chain, `dealOf` at that block must not show an earlier stage than the one this node last applied, or the pass waits a block. A later stage is taken as the contract's (red team, `attack.t231-behind-recovery-stale-stage`): a restart's recovery reads `dealOf` at the latest block, so a lagging node there can leave the book's stage behind, and the pass would otherwise wait on it for good. The relay's own simulation and that recovery read stay unpinned: both are T-232's (D54).
- 2026-11-06, review and red team: **a minute's grace past each deadline.** The brief asked for each timeout "as soon as it's due". Both reviews found that this races a party who is there. Round 1's scenario 3a sends the buyer's own reclaim 2 s after `deliverBy`; whichever send loses reverts on chain (47,600 gas, measured by the red team), and when the driver loses, its scenario fails. With the grace the party sends first, and the operators' check then finds the contract refusing and sends nothing (`attack.t231-round1-direct-reclaim-preempted`). Deals still close with no caller, one to two minutes after the deadline instead of within one (inferred). `graceMs` is an option, 60,000 by default.
- 2026-11-06, red team: **no send while a lane row is open.** A reclaim mined while every read at its block failed left its row open, the book Held and the next pass free to send it again, which reverted (47,600 gas, measured). The pass now leaves alone any deal with an open lane row (`LiveChain.openRows`, all rows written and not yet released), not only those a restart left: the sync or the recovery applies that receipt first.
- 2026-11-06, review and red team: **the budget is given back when nothing went out,** and every deal pauses after the relay refuses. A refusal before the broadcast, or a timeout someone else ran first, used to spend a unit (6 units in 8 passes with 0 sends, measured). Now the unit comes back (`TimeoutBudget.give`), and after a `relay_refused` (out of test ETH, gas above the cap) no deal is tried for a minute, doubling to 15 minutes.
- 2026-11-06: **a late trigger answers its result.** `EvmSettlement.timeout` returns the current state, with no send, when the deal's log already holds that timeout's event (`offer.expired`, `settlement.reclaimed`, `fallback.triggered`, `default.triggered`, `appeal.expired`), as `apply_default` already did. Without it a party's trigger that comes after the operators' answers 409 `invalid_state: contract revert WrongStage(6)` (measured, below), which fails round 1's driver. A deal behind the chain logs no event for its later steps, so the contract still answers for it, as in T-228. SimChain is unchanged: no pass sends timeouts there.
- 2026-11-06: **the Idempotency-Key** is `operator-timeout:` plus the canonical keccak of the timeout, the commitment and the world's secret. The settlement layer stores keys across all commitments, and a client chooses its own key. A guessable key would let a client post another trigger under it first, so the operators' send would meet `idempotency_conflict`.
- 2026-11-06: **the budget.** 200 timeouts a UTC day, with offer expiries capped at 50 inside it. One row `ot:<day>` is written before each send, as the faucet counts its mints, so a restart cannot reset it, and a unit comes back when nothing went out. Past the budget a deal waits for the next UTC day or a party's trigger. The first cut was 300 and 100, sized on 80,792 gas a timeout (round 1's relayed appeal expiry, T-228). The red team measured more on anvil: a reclaim 140,019 gas, a default 125,424 and an offer expiry 65,597. At 140,019 gas each, 200 a day is at most about 28 million gas: 0.028 test ETH at 1 gwei a gas, or 0.56 at the relayer's 20 gwei cap (inferred). Sepolia's own figures are not measured here.
- 2026-11-06: **the griefer's cost.** A griefer who opens funded deals designed to time out pays the 4.71 tUSDC operator fee on each. The fee is not enough, and the daily budget, not the fee, bounds what T-231 spends (the red team agrees).
  - In the demo's own units the fee covers the gas. The 4.70 tUSDC quote prices about 1.56 million gas (measured on Sepolia, T-213). Such a deal costs the operators the funding they relay, 840,709 gas (measured, T-228's smoke deal), plus at most two timeouts, about 280,000 gas (a takeover and a default at the dearest figure measured, 140,019 each; the takeover itself was not measured). That is about 1.1 million gas in all (inferred).
  - In real terms the fee is worth nothing. tUSDC is an open-mint test token with no value, so on Sepolia the fee costs a griefer nothing, and only the relayer's Sepolia ETH is at stake.
  - T-231 adds about a third to what such a deal already costs the relayer (280,000 of 840,709 gas, inferred): the funding relay is the main cost, and it existed before T-231, with no daily cap. The budget caps what the pass adds at about 28 million gas a day.
  - The budget is first come, first served: a griefer can spend a day's 200 and make honest deals wait for the next UTC day. Their parties can still trigger their own timeouts at no gas in the meantime. A cap per buyer would be fairer; it is not built (Handoff).
  - On mainnet with USDC the fee is real money, and D33 sets it to cover the gas.
  - A low balance on the funding lane is the backstop: since D54, Crow Control's "Waiting on you" warns when the funding lane holds less than about 200 deals of runway (crow-private PR 6), and the full alarm stays in T-054.
- 2026-11-06, red team: **offer expiries stay, within their cap.** The red team showed that on a chain an expired offer holds no money and no bond: the acceptance reserves nothing, and past either deadline `agreeAndFund` reverts `DeadlinePassed`. An offer expiry spent 65,597 gas and changed only what `dealOf` reads (`attack.t231-offer-expiry-spends-gas-for-nothing`, measured). It proposed dropping them. They stay, because the brief asks that every deal reach its end: without the expiry an abandoned offer reads `AcceptedByAdjudicator` with no `finalAt` for good. The cost is bounded at 50 a day, about 3.3 million gas (inferred). The test is not kept, since it asserts the opposite decision.
- 2026-11-06, review: **the takeover at `fallbackBy` hands the fallback its key envelope,** as every takeover does (`custody`), though that fallback can no longer decide. It is the fallback the parties named in their terms, which a takeover at `judgeBy` would have handed the same envelope. Changing the custody rule would change T-218's and T-228's paths too, so this task keeps it and records it.
- 2026-11-06: **one send after another.** The pass sends at most 4 timeouts, each waiting for its block (about 12 s on Sepolia), so a pass takes at most about 48 s plus the reads. A Durable Object alarm may run for 15 minutes (guessed from Cloudflare's published limits; not checked here). Lanes in parallel would be faster, but no demo load needs that.
