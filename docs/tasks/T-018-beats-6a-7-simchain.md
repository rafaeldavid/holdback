## Intent

Make the demo's two strongest claims real. Beat 7 shows an operator refusing and the primary adjudicator going silent, and the deal still settling. Beat 6a shows a base64 injection that the screen flags, the case escalating, and the appeal panel rejecting it. Today both are stand-in fiction, and beat 6a cannot happen because of review finding L3-1.

**Restated (implementer, 2026-10-31).** Turn the two scripted beats into runs on SimChain that a researcher can trust. Beat 6a: a seller hides a base64 instruction to the judges; the screen flags it, the primary escalates, the appeal panel decides and refunds the buyer. That needs a defense fix first: today a flagged case escalates at every role, so the appeal never signs a decision and the default outcome pays the injecting seller from the appeal bond (L3-1). At the appeal the flag must stay in the record while the panel decides by supermajority, never auto-accepting; the primary and fallback keep escalating. Beat 7: an operator refuses through its own policy and the agents still settle (failover, then direct submission); the primary goes silent and anyone moves the case to the fallback, which decides. Then delete the stand-in so no transcript carries fiction.

Non-goals: club revocation at run time (SimChain has none; the beat drops it and says so), live judges, T-016 runner work, T-019's labels, IDs, timing and prompt display, and any schema change.

## Decisions

- **D27b/c (red-team re-check), under D28.**
  - `aggregate` takes `appeal: 'escalated' | 'party'` (pipeline `appealKind`, set by the service and the snap runner from `firstOutcome`).
  - After an escalation, a sampled read is one more seller-controllable condition under the strict rule.
  - On a party appeal every such condition escalates and `signedOutcome` keeps the first decision.
  - The `agg-3` digest is re-pinned without an `agg-4` (`agg-3` has not reached `main`), and it now also covers `signedOutcome`.
- **D27 replaces the first L3-1 rule (D25).** After the red-team review, a flagged appeal (screen flag, `injectionSuspected`, or a filter abstention) accepts only on a unanimous valid panel from the appeal family minimum with no injection suspected and the score met, and otherwise rejects; it never escalates. Primary and fallback unchanged. It keys on the pipeline's `template` (`appeal`, `appealFamilies` from the policy's family minimum, `filterAbstentions`), so the service and snap share it, and both sign through `signedOutcome`. A sampled (strided) appeal also counts against accept. The empty-rubric accept with no panel (ADR-0021) is unchanged at every role. `AGGREGATION_VERSION` is `agg-3`, pinned to a decision-table digest. Recorded in ADR-0021 (amendment), FR-15, `docs/judgment-and-defenses.md` and D27.
- `OperatorOptions.policy` receives a frozen deep copy of the operation and a `PolicyContext` (parties from the operation or the node's index, and the signer); `refuseParties` answers 403 `relay_refused`, a new operator-only problem type (`packages/core/src/problem.ts`, `docs/api.md`; not in `spec/schemas`).
- Beat 7 adds op-2 going offline after the hold, so "submits directly" is measured, not only failover. The world gained `refusals`, `operatorLog` and `stopOperator`; benches take per-bench scripts (`benchJudges`).
- Beat 6 is two jobs on two worlds; the second job's times are shifted after the first's and its balance labels say "second delivery". The source note says so.
- Renderer and narration tests read measured transcripts (`apps/demo/src/source/measured.fixture.ts`) instead of the stand-in. After the T-019 merge the decision line carries the judging time, so the `decision` exemption is gone; the Markdown panel section names each panel's adjudicator when a job has two; the scripted-mode header says verdicts are written in advance (pass unless the beat scripts a fail); `beatHappyPath` takes the negotiation `observe` option, so the source's `happyJob` no longer duplicates it.
- After the T-019 merge each decision reads its own report (record, judge requests, judging marks), since beat 6a decides twice; the record comparison resolves every published record and skips an offline operator.
- `attack.injection-encoded-base64` is the beat 6a snap fixture (restored from `todo.` after PR 50): real CSV, Bench A escalates on the screen flag although its fake judges pass, Bench C's five authored fails with injection suspected, Refunded. With fake pass verdicts at the appeal it ends Released, which D27a allows only for a unanimous, three-family, no-injection panel.
- The service and the snap runner now share `signedOutcome` from `@holdback/judgment` instead of two copies of the appeal mapping (T-016 handoff).
