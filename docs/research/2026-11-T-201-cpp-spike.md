# T-201 spike: can a Holdback rules contract operate CPP's `AuthCaptureEscrow`?

- **Date:** 2026-11-01
- **Task:** `docs/tasks/T-201-cpp-spike.md` (D3, ADR-0003; D4 update of 2026-11-01)
- **Code:** `contracts/` (Foundry). CPP vendored with `forge install base/commerce-payments@v1.1.0`, pinned at `6b0a5ef3f0df973857f558e2d6b5dd645b5e97f9`, unmodified. `contracts/src/HoldbackRules.sol` is a minimal operator; tests are in `contracts/test/`.
- **Where it ran:** `forge test` in Foundry's in-process EVM (revm, chain id 31337). No anvil node was started; no transaction was broadcast; no public RPC was called. Block-explorer pages were read for question 6 only.

Labels: **measured** (a test or command on this machine, or a page I read), **inferred** (follows from measured facts), **guessed** (an estimate).

## Recommendation

**Keep option A**: `AuthCaptureEscrow` v1.1.0 for custody, with a no-admin Holdback rules contract as its operator. Do not fall back to B (ERC-8183) or C (custom). [inferred from the answers below]

- A contract with no admin drove every operator path a deal needs: fund, accept, early accept, fee-only reject, timeout. The buyer's reclaim after expiry worked with no operator at all. [measured: 36 tests pass]
- Nothing found needs a change to CPP. Every gap is in the rules contract, as ADR-0003 expected: permissionless wrappers, the settle-once guard, the fee rule, the deadline margin. [measured, inferred]
- The pinned commit is the exact commit of the July 2026 Cantina review, and the Base deployment's metadata hash matches our build of it. [measured]

Three findings change the Phase 2 plan, but none argues for B or C:

1. **CPP is not deployed on Ethereum mainnet or Sepolia.** It is deployed only on Base mainnet and Base Sepolia. Under D4, Holdback deploys the same audited bytecode itself. That is permissionless and reproducible (question 6). [measured]
2. **Gas.** A deal costs about 256k to 273k gas over two transactions. At 1 gwei and $3,000 per ETH, that is about $0.80 per deal, about 80 times the D33 minimum fee of 0.01 tUSD. D4 said to revisit the chain choice on this evidence. [measured gas; guessed prices]
3. **Funding message.** On chain, the buyer's funding signature is an ERC-3009 `ReceiveWithAuthorization` with fields fixed by CPP. It differs from today's `FundingAuthorization` (`validAfter` must be 0, and the nonce is derived). T-202 needs a schema ADR (question 7). [measured]

## The seven questions

### 1. Can a no-admin contract be CPP's operator, so that only Holdback's rules move funds?

**Yes.** [measured]

- `HoldbackRules` calls `authorize`, `capture` and `void` as `PaymentInfo.operator`. CPP checks only `msg.sender == operator` (`AuthCaptureEscrow.sol` L170-173). [measured: `test_happyPath_*`, `test_reject_*`, `test_timeout_*`]
- Funds move on exactly three triggers, and anyone can relay each one:
  - `decide`: the named adjudicator's EIP-712 decision;
  - `earlyAccept`: the buyer's EIP-712 early accept;
  - `timeout`: anyone, once `decideBy` has passed.
  
  A wrong signer, a flipped outcome, or a decision for another commitment reverts. [measured: `test_decide_wrongSigner_reverts`, `test_decide_signatureForOtherOutcome_reverts`, `test_decide_signatureForOtherCommitment_reverts`, `test_earlyAccept_notByBuyer_reverts`, `test_timeout_beforeDecideBy_reverts`]
- **Funding needs a permissionless wrapper.** CPP's `authorize` is operator-only, so `HoldbackRules.fund` relays the buyer's signature for anyone. [measured: `test_fund_anyRelayerSubmitsBuyerSignature_fundsAreHeld`]
  - The buyer's signature binds the operator: the ERC-3009 nonce is CPP's payer-agnostic hash of `PaymentInfo`, and `operator` is one of its fields. Another operator cannot use the signature. [measured: `test_fund_signatureCannotBeUsedByAnotherOperator`]
  - It also binds every agreed term: `salt` is the EIP-712 hash of the Holdback `Terms`, so a relayer that swaps the adjudicator fails. [measured: `test_fund_signatureForOtherTermsIsRejected`]
- **No admin.** Checked in three ways [measured: `NoAdmin.t.sol`]:
  - the compiled ABI matches an exact allowlist of 13 selectors;
  - the contract has no `fallback` or `receive`;
  - 14 common admin selectors (`owner`, `pause`, `upgradeTo`, `grantRole`, `withdraw` …) all fail.
  
  The rules contract never holds tokens. Custody stays in CPP's per-operator `TokenStore` clone.
- **One residual for the neutrality test** (the hold: inferred, measured mechanics). `void` has no deadline in CPP, so the operator could void at any time. Only `HoldbackRules`' code prevents it. Neutrality therefore rests on the rules contract being immutable, with no admin and correct code. That is the audit surface Holdback owns (ADR-0003 Consequences). [measured: CPP L304; inferred]

### 2. Capture timing and expiry

- **Funds can stay held until a decision.** A hold captured after 364 days worked. [measured: `test_holdStaysCapturableForAYear`]
- **The expiry has no cap.** `authorizationExpiry` is a `uint48` timestamp, and an expiry at `type(uint48).max` (about the year 8.9 million) worked, which satisfies D31. [measured: `test_farFutureExpiry_isRepresentable`]
- **The deadlines are fixed when the buyer signs.** `holdExpiry` (CPP `authorizationExpiry` and `refundExpiry`) is part of the signed `PaymentInfo`. It cannot be extended for an appeal, so the worst-case path (deliver, judge, fallback, appeal) plus a margin must fit before it. [inferred from CPP L383-386]
- **A decision that lands at or after `holdExpiry` cannot capture.** The buyer then reclaims, even with a signed accept in hand. [measured: `test_decisionAfterHoldExpiry_cannotCapture_buyerReclaims`]
  - So the margin protects the seller: anyone, the seller included, can relay the decision, but it must land in time. [inferred]
  - On Ethereum L1 there is no sequencer to force through, so the margin only has to cover congestion and short-lived censorship. A default of one day or more is a guess. Under D31 the parties set it; `fund` refuses only `decideBy > holdExpiry`. [guessed; measured: `test_fund_holdExpiryBeforeDecideBy_reverts`]
- **Timeouts.** The spike's default outcome is a refund via `void`, and `void` works before and after `holdExpiry`, so a refund default never strands. [measured: `test_timeout_anyoneAfterDecideBy_refundsBuyerInFull`; inferred for the post-expiry case from CPP L304-316]
  - A seller-favourable default (D5, paid from the bond) needs `capture` and therefore must fire before `holdExpiry`. [inferred]
- **Settlement modes.**
  - `guaranteed_on_decision` captures at the decision, as the spike does. [measured]
  - `settle_after_window` can leave the funds authorized until the appeal window closes, because `capture` works at any time before `authorizationExpiry`. [inferred from the one-year test]
  - The key-release gap in that mode (ADR-0012) is unchanged.

### 3. The refund path

- **The fee-only capture works.** On reject, `capture(amount = fee, feeAmount = fee, feeReceiver = adjudicator)` sends the fee to the adjudicator and nothing to the seller, because CPP sends `amount - feeAmount = 0` to the receiver. `void` then returns the rest to the buyer. [measured: `test_reject_capturesOnlyTheFee_andRefundsTheRest`]
  - This requires `maxFeeBps = 10000`. [measured: CPP `_validateFee`]
- **Edge cases.**
  - Fee 0: plain `void`, full refund. [measured: `test_reject_withZeroFee_voidsEverything`]
  - Fee equal to the price: an all-fee capture with no `void`. [measured: `test_reject_feeEqualToPrice_capturesAllAsFee`]
- **The payer can reclaim after expiry with no operator action.** The buyer called `escrow.reclaim` directly, and `HoldbackRules` was never touched. [measured: `test_payerReclaim_afterExpiry_withNoOperatorAction`]
  - Reclaim reverts before `holdExpiry`, and only the payer can call it. [measured: same test and `test_reclaim_isPayerOnly`]

### 4. Does the rules contract need its own replay protection?

**Yes, for settlement. No, for funding.** [measured]

- **Funding.** CPP already refuses a second `authorize` for the same `PaymentInfo` (`PaymentAlreadyCollected`), and the ERC-3009 nonce is single-use. [measured: `test_fund_replayReverts`; CPP L247]
- **Settlement.** CPP bounds the total captured by the hold, not the number or shape of captures. With `feeReceiver = 0` and `maxFeeBps = 10000`, an operator can call `capture` repeatedly and route each slice, or the whole hold, to any address as a "fee". [measured: `test_cppAlone_allowsRepeatedAllFeeCapturesToAnyAddress`]
  - `HoldbackRules` therefore keeps a per-terms `Stage` (None → Held → Settled), and every settlement path passes through `_settleOnce`.
  - A replayed decision, a conflicting second decision, and a timeout after a decision all revert `WrongStage(Settled)`. [measured: `test_decide_replay_revertsAlreadySettled`, `test_conflictingDecisions_firstWins`]
- **Off-chain idempotency.** The API's `Idempotency-Key` stays an operator-node concern. On chain, the settle-once guard plus CPP's funding guard give the same result: one outcome per deal. [inferred]

### 5. Fees: CPP's fee fields, the adjudication fee and the operator fee

- **Settings.** `PaymentInfo` is set to `minFeeBps = 0`, `maxFeeBps = 10000` and `feeReceiver = 0`, as ADR-0003 planned. [measured: `test_fund_paymentInfoBindsOperatorAndTerms`]
  - `feeReceiver` must be 0, because the fee goes to whichever adjudicator decides (primary, fallback or appeal).
  - `maxFeeBps` must be 10000, because a reject captures the fee alone. [inferred]
- **What protects the buyer.** CPP's fee bounds give the buyer no protection under these settings. The protection is the rules contract's code:
  - the fee is the absolute `adjudicationFee` from the signed `Terms`, paid only to `Terms.adjudicator`, and at most once (question 4);
  - `fund` refuses `adjudicationFee > price`. [measured: code and tests; inferred]
- **Fee splitting.** On accept, CPP splits price and fee in one `capture`: `price - fee` to the seller and `fee` to the adjudicator. [measured: `test_happyPath_*`]
- **The D33 operator fee stays outside the escrow, with no interaction.**
  - It is a separate ERC-3009 transfer from the buyer to the relaying operator's address, with its own nonce. [inferred from ADR-0023 and the ERC-3009 nonce scheme]
  - The CPP collector uses `receiveWithAuthorization`, where the payee must be the caller, and its nonce is derived from `PaymentInfo`, so the two signatures cannot collide. [inferred]
  - The spike did not run the operator-fee transfer, because the CPP mock token implements only `receiveWithAuthorization`. [measured]
- **Audit note on fees.** The July 2026 review's informational finding 3.1.2 (capture fragmentation rounds the minimum fee to zero) does not apply, because `minFeeBps = 0`. [measured: audit p.4; inferred]

### 7. Funding: ERC-3009 and Permit2 against `FundingAuthorization`

- **ERC-3009 works** with CPP's `MockERC3009Token` (USDC's typehash and domain name "USD Coin", version "2"). [measured: every funded test]
- **What the buyer signs on chain** is the token's `ReceiveWithAuthorization`, with the fields fixed by CPP's collector (`ERC3009PaymentCollector.sol`):

  | Field | Value |
  | --- | --- |
  | `from` | buyer |
  | `to` | the ERC-3009 collector |
  | `value` | `maxAmount` (the price) |
  | `validAfter` | 0 |
  | `validBefore` | `preApprovalExpiry` (`fundBy`) |
  | `nonce` | the payer-agnostic `PaymentInfo` hash |

- **It does not match today's `FundingAuthorization`** (`packages/core/src/types.ts`; EIP-712 fields `scheme, commitmentId, from, value, token, validAfter, validBefore`):
  - a non-zero `validAfter` fails [measured: `test_fund_erc3009WithNonZeroValidAfter_isRejected`];
  - the nonce must be derived, not random;
  - the token is an address, not a symbol.
  
  T-202 should make the on-chain funding signature the ERC-3009 struct above, and have the API derive and show it from the agreed terms. The commitment is bound through `salt = hash(Terms)`. That is a schema change, so it needs an ADR and a version bump. [inferred]
- **Permit2** works the same way in CPP: `Permit2PaymentCollector` uses the same payer-agnostic hash as the Permit2 nonce and `preApprovalExpiry` as the deadline. It suits tokens without ERC-3009. [measured: source; not run in this spike, but covered by CPP's own suite]
- **Sepolia.** Circle's USDC implements ERC-3009, so Sepolia testing can use real test USDC. [inferred; not checked on chain]

## Gas per deal

How it was measured: `forge test --match-contract GasPerDeal --isolate -vv`. `--isolate` runs each top-level call as its own transaction, so the numbers include 21,000 intrinsic gas, calldata cost and cold-access costs. The numbers match `forge test --gas-report --isolate` for `fund` and `decide`. They come from Foundry's EVM, not from anvil receipts. [measured]

"Steady" means the rules contract's `TokenStore` clone already exists and the seller and adjudicator already hold the token.

| Path | Transaction 1 (`fund`) | Transaction 2 (settle) | Total |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Happy path, adjudicator accept | 176,842 | 89,694 | **266,536** |
| Happy path, buyer early accept (no judge) | 176,842 | 78,935 | **255,777** |
| Refund path, adjudicator reject (fee-only capture + `void`) | 176,842 | 95,875 | **272,717** |
| Refund path, permissionless timeout (`void`) | 176,842 | 68,037 | **244,879** |
| Backstop, buyer `reclaim` after expiry | 176,842 | 51,885 | **228,727** |
| First deal ever, adjudicator accept (deploys the `TokenStore` clone) | 176,842 | 166,747 | **343,589** |

[measured]

- **Two transactions per deal**, which meets the D4 target of one funding and one settlement transaction on the happy path. [measured]
- **Real USDC costs more.** Real USDC (a FiatToken proxy with denylist checks) costs more per transfer than the Solady-based mock. Funding makes three transfers (to the collector, then to the store, plus a return of any excess), and settlement makes one or two. Expect roughly +10k to +25k gas per deal. [guessed]
- **What it costs in money.**

  | Gas price, ETH price | Happy path |
  | --- | --- |
  | 1 gwei, $3,000 | about $0.80 |
  | 10 gwei, $3,000 | about $8 |

  The D33 operator fee (minimum 0.01 tUSD) does not cover the relayer's gas at either price. [guessed prices; measured gas]
- **Option C would save a little.** A custom escrow could skip the collector hop and the clone call, perhaps 20 to 30% (about 50k to 80k gas). That is not enough to give up audited custody code. [guessed]

## What T-202 inherits

1. Permissionless `fund`, `decide`, `earlyAccept` and `timeout` wrappers, so that anyone can relay any signed message. [measured]
2. `salt = hash(Terms)`, so that one buyer signature binds the operator, the terms and the funding. [measured]
3. The settle-once guard. CPP does not provide one. [measured]
4. A fixed `holdExpiry` covering the worst-case path plus a margin chosen by the parties. A seller-favourable default must fire before it. [inferred]
5. The schema change to the funding message (question 7), through an ADR. [inferred]
6. A deployment plan for CPP on Sepolia and later mainnet, from the pinned commit with the `deploy` profile, checked by the metadata hash. [inferred]
7. The gas evidence for the D4 revisit. [measured]
