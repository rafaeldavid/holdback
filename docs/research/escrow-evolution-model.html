      A protocol designer deciding what to standardize for agent-to-agent commitments needs to know whether such commitments exist in production, at which tier, and what stops them from growing. The answer: AI agents make commitments today at every point on the gradient from conditional triggers to payments, but the counterparty is almost never another agent. As of October 29, 2026, production commitments run at volume in three settings: binding B2B agreements in which an agent negotiates with a human (Pactum for Walmart; C.H. Robinson and HappyRobot in freight brokerage), consumer and B2B payments in which the agent spends inside a card-network or platform envelope, and machine micropayments on crypto rails. Commitments in which both parties are LLM agents are documented in two narrow pockets: AdCP media buys, where a human on the sell side still approves each campaign and the largest disclosed volume is about $1 million (Apostra, as reported by PPC Land on October 24, 2026),1 and on-chain machine markets such as Olas, whose live counter showed 14.9 million agent-to-agent transactions against $110,425 in lifetime turnover on October 29, 2026.2 Escrowed and conditional payment between agents exists as specifications (ERC-8183, Virtuals ACP, the x402 deferred scheme, AP2 open mandates) with observable use in the single-digit millions of dollars (about $5.6 million in job-marketplace escrow flows from registered agents, by arithmetic on an on-chain analysis dated October 20, 2026).3 Across all of it, commitment strength and the likelihood that the counterparty is an agent move in opposite directions: the larger and more binding the commitment, the more likely the other side is a human or an incumbent institution that absorbs residual risk. Operator identity is no longer the binding constraint. The unsolved layers are intent integrity, meaning proof that an authenticated agent's specific commitment reflects what its principal wanted, and allocation of liability for errors made inside an agent's authorized scope. For infrastructure and product decisions, the evidence favors building signed offer, acceptance and remedy semantics on top of existing transport protocols, enforcing loss bounds outside the model, and entering through pre-contracted B2B relationships rather than open agent marketplaces.

      The design problem: announcements outnumber commitments

      The designer faces an evidence problem before a design problem. The public record in late 2026 is dominated by membership counts, protocol launches and transaction tallies that do not measure commitments. The Linux Foundation reported on April 9, 2026 that more than 150 organizations support the A2A protocol and claimed "production deployments across multiple industries," but the release names no production user.4 An independent review dated June 24, 2026 found no named enterprise running cross-vendor A2A in production and concluded that "A2A is alive but its useful scope is narrower than the hype suggests."5 American Express's head of global innovation said in April 2026 that "to date there have probably been as many press releases as transactions."6 On crypto rails, Chainalysis's October 14, 2026 report, as cited by the Motley Fool on October 24, 2026, counted 138.5 million x402 payments on Base, while the Visa and Artemis report of July 16, 2026, as reported by KuCoin News that day, put adjusted cumulative volume near $15 million.78

      Misreading this record has a cost. If agent-to-agent commerce were already large, the priority would be throughput, discovery and settlement finality. If it is small and confined to bounded settings, the priority is the layers that would let it grow: who is bound, what was promised, how performance is verified and who pays when an authorized agent commits wrongly. Choosing the wrong layer means standardizing plumbing that already exists while the constraint sits elsewhere. This report separates what is observed in production from what is specified, piloted or forecast, then builds a framework and an argument from the observations.

      Three conventions apply throughout. Measured refers to a count produced by a party other than the vendor selling the product, or to an on-chain figure an analyst filtered. Vendor self-report refers to a company describing its own product. Forecast refers to a projection. The research notes behind this report were compiled on October 29, 2026; several planned lookups (Workday, Oracle, insurance claims, prior-authorization voice agents, Booking Holdings, Amadeus, and whether the Sabre–Mindtrip flight booking went live in Q2 2026) were not completed, and absence of evidence in those areas is not evidence of absence.

      Observation: production commitments cluster where an institution already absorbs the risk

      The gradient in the question runs from conditional commitments (if X, do Y) through reservations and holds, binding agreements and payments, to escrowed or conditional payment. Each tier is examined below for what is committed, who the counterparty is, how the commitment is enforced and what volume is documented.

      Conditional commitments: no agent protocol expresses do Y when X

      The simplest commitment, an agent agreeing to act when a condition becomes true, has no standard representation between agents. The A2A specification (v1.0, March 12, 2026; patch 1.0.1, May 26, 2026) defines eight task states, signed Agent Cards and per-task webhooks, but webhook delivery is an at-least-once attempt that the server may abandon after repeated failures, and the initial state, TASK_STATE_SUBMITTED, means only that a task was received and acknowledged.9 Cancellation is best-effort; in the specification's words, "The server will attempt to cancel the task, but success is not guaranteed." A full-text search of the specification found no service-level, SLA or contractual commitment language.9 The Model Context Protocol revision of July 28, 2026 moved Tasks out of core into an extension, and the extension makes notifications optional and states that a server receiving a cancellation is not obligated to stop work.1011 MCP's August 22, 2026 roadmap lists webhooks and a Triggers and Events working group as future work, not shipped features.12

      The mechanisms that exist run in the opposite direction from a conditional commitment. A2A and MCP subscriptions let a caller hear about the callee's own task; they do not let a caller obligate a callee to act when an external condition holds. The FIPA Communicative Act Library (2002) defined exactly that primitive, request-when, under which the receiver must either refuse the commitment or ensure the action happens once the condition becomes true, with explicit discharge and abandonment semantics.13 Current protocols dropped it. Agents that need if X then Y today either hold an A2A task open with the condition stated in natural language, which carries no protocol meaning, or delegate the trigger to an automation platform exposed as an MCP tool; Zapier reports 9,000 apps and 66,000 triggers and actions available to agents (vendor self-report).14

      Two deployed products come closest to a signed conditional commitment, and both are principal-to-agent authorizations rather than agent-to-agent obligations. AP2's human-not-present mode, released in v0.2 on April 28, 2026, lets a user pre-sign an open mandate with constraints (merchants, budget, date ranges, recurrence) bound to a specific agent key; the agent later signs a closed mandate when the conditions match, and verifiers check it against the open constraints.15 Amazon's Rufus Auto Buy, extended to US Prime members in late November 2025, buys when a price target is hit and gives the shopper 24 hours to cancel before shipping.16 Both authorize an agent to act on a condition. Neither obligates a counterparty agent to do anything.

      Reservations and holds: thousands per day, mostly over human channels

      Reservation-level commitments, which hold a slot or resource and can be released under known norms, run at scale but mostly over channels built for people. C.H. Robinson's June 12, 2025 newsroom release reports that its Appointments Agent books more than 3,000 dock appointments a day across 43,000 locations (company-reported).17 Google's consumer calling agents moved from restaurant reservations under Duplex, which VentureBeat reported on March 6, 2019 had reached 43 US states, to information-only calls under Ask for Me in February 2025, to a September 2026 Gemini Call for Me beta that books tables and reschedules appointments for Pixel 11 subscribers in the US; no volumes are published for any of them.1819 The counterparty in each case is a human or a facility system, possibly an AI receptionist, but no published report documents a measured case of one AI agent's phone call being answered by another. Gartner's projection, cited by CX Today on February 3, 2025, that "machine customers" would generate 20% of contact-center traffic by 2026 is a forecast, and the notes contain no measurement against it.20

      The one reservation-tier mechanism designed for agent counterparties sits in advertising. AdCP v3 (April 22, 2026) gives insertion orders a submitted state that can last hours or days while a human sales team reviews the buy, and requires human review for housing, lending, employment and pharmaceutical categories.1 That is a hold between two agents, gated by a person.

      Binding agreements: the largest commitments are made with humans

      The best-documented case of an AI agent forming binding commercial agreements in production is Pactum's work for Walmart, and its counterparty is a human supplier. In a three-month Walmart Canada pilot with tail-end, goods-not-for-resale suppliers, described in Harvard Business Review in November 2022 and summarized by Talking Logistics on May 1, 2023, 89 suppliers took part and 64% reached agreement against a 20% target, with average savings of 1.5% and payment terms extended by an average of 35 days.21 Bloomberg, as reported by PYMNTS in April 2023, put the closure rate at 68% and average savings at 3%, with nearly three in four suppliers preferring the bot.22 MIT Sloan Management Review (c. 2023) reported 7–10% savings after expansion to mid-tier suppliers in several countries.23 These savings figures come from different phases, supplier tiers and baselines and should not be merged. Pactum's Series C release of June 9, 2025 reports a largest agent-negotiated deal of $140.5 million and a fastest deal "negotiated and signed by agents in 87 seconds" (vendor self-report).24 The mechanism is a delegated envelope: Walmart sets budgets and trade-off rules, the bot offers payment-term extensions, early payment or termination rights in exchange for price, and the human supplier's acceptance forms the contract. No Walmart or Pactum figures published after 2023 were found.

      Freight brokerage produces the largest volume of binding agent commitments, again mostly with human counterparties. C.H. Robinson's June 2025 release reports more than 1 million quotes generated in 32 seconds each and about 5,500 truckload orders a day built from emailed tenders, each in about 90 seconds (company-reported); FreightWaves reported on April 16, 2025 that more than 5,200 small and mid-size shippers had loads accepted in under 90 seconds, against up to four hours before.1725 HappyRobot's undated customer story for Circle Logistics reports that voice agents answer all inbound carrier calls, negotiate rates, confirm bookings and run fraud checks, and that 18% of freight is booked with no human touch (vendor self-report).26 On the other side of the table, FreightWaves reported on September 28, 2026 that Hey Bubba's carrier-side agent negotiates with 5–10 brokers at once by phone, email and API.27 Broker-side and carrier-side agents therefore both exist, and HappyRobot is described as serving eight of the ten largest US brokers (AI-authored secondary source, Forkast, August 20, 2026).28 Agent-to-agent freight negotiations are likely already happening without anyone labeling them. No published report confirms one, and no protocol lets the two agents recognize each other.

      Advertising is the one sector where both parties to a priced, binding commitment are documented as agents. AdCP launched on October 15, 2025 with Yahoo, PubMatic, Scope3 and others as founding members.29 Magnite reported on January 6, 2026 that its seller agent ran its first AdCP test in December 2025 with Scope3 as the buyer agent, and called the effort "far too early to predict outcomes."30 On December 15, 2025, PubMatic announced that an agency brief submitted through Anthropic's Claude was interpreted by PubMatic's agents, which built and launched a connected-TV campaign for Clubtails; spend and results were not disclosed.31 PPC Land reported in June 2026 that an Omnicom buyer agent running through ChatGPT dealt server-to-server with R2B2's sell-side gateway to buy space on the Czech publisher MAFRA for Skylink, with MAFRA manually approving every campaign and creative.32 PPC Land's October 24, 2026 status review reports that Apostra (formerly Scope3) disclosed about $1 million in agentic campaign volume, much of it from one advertiser, and that Google, Amazon, The Trade Desk and Microsoft are not founding members.1 The commitments are binding and the counterparties are agents; the volume is a rounding error next to programmatic display, which an undated eMarketer analysis (c. 2024) put at more than $135 billion of US spend in 2023.33

      Enterprise agent-to-agent traffic carries information, not commitments. The only named cross-company A2A deployment, a pilot connecting Gordon Food Service agents with supplier Tyson Foods' agents, covers product search and sales tracking; no orders, quotes or binding actions are described, and the undated customer page says only that GFS plans to move it to production.34 Salesforce's Q2 FY2027 earnings call, as summarized by MarketBeat on August 26, 2026, reported 3.2 billion agentic work units, up 97% quarter over quarter, with no mention of cross-vendor agent interoperability.35 Commitment-bearing actions in enterprise software (refunds, appointments, orders) happen inside one vendor's agent against that vendor's own systems of record.

      Payments: the envelope lives in the card network

      Agent payments to merchants are live at every major platform and network, with a human confirmation step in most consumer flows and no published volumes. OpenAI launched Instant Checkout on September 29, 2025 on the Agentic Commerce Protocol co-developed with Stripe.36 Forrester's March 7, 2026 analysis and Shopifreaks' March 12, 2026 report, both citing The Information, put live Shopify merchants at about a dozen (Shopify told Forrester the figure was closer to 30) and buy-button conversion below 1%, and Shopifreaks reported on March 9, 2026 that OpenAI had not built systems to collect and remit state sales tax; OpenAI said on March 6, 2026 that "Instant Checkout is moving to Apps."37383940 The payment primitive survived the product. Stripe's Shared Payment Token is scoped to one seller with a currency, maximum amount and expiry, can be revoked by its issuer, and since March 3, 2026 can carry Visa and Mastercard agentic network tokens.4142

      The card networks moved the spending envelope into the token. Mastercard's Agentic Tokens are issued per agent, carry scope set at issuance and can be revoked without affecting the card; according to Mastercard announcements compiled by BankingNewsAI (accessed October 2026), Mastercard enabled all European issuers at network level on June 2, 2026, with a first production payment in the Netherlands through Worldline and ING, and it has published no transaction counts.4344 Visa reported "hundreds" of agent-initiated transactions on December 18, 2025, the only network-published count in the record, and announced live European merchant purchases with more than 30 issuers on July 2, 2026 (per the same BankingNewsAI compilation).4546 Visa's Core Rules of April 18, 2026 add Agentic Platform Requirements: provider registration, cardholder consent to tokenize and to the payment instruction, a stated expiry and a cardholder acknowledgement of responsibility for the provider's actions (reported through secondary summaries; the rules PDF was not opened).47 On October 20, 2026, Visa, BILL and Billtrust launched the first live B2B pilot, in which BILL's registered agent pays supplier invoices through Billtrust portals, each payment tied to a specific invoice the buyer has already approved.48

      Corporate-card vendors bound agent spending at card issuance. Ramp's Agent Cards (April 28, 2026) are scoped to a single agent and transaction, merchant-locked with a hard cap, and backed by single-use Visa network tokens (vendor self-report).49 Mercury's agent cards auto-decline out-of-rule transactions and do not let the agent raise its own limit (vendor self-report).50 Natural's Vaults, launched in July 2026, let an agent deposit into accounts but not withdraw, a structural bound on loss.51 None of these firms disclosed volume.

      The demand side is thin. Visa's survey with Morning Consult (April 2, 2026) found 60% of US adults would not let AI spend any amount without approval; Mastercard's Signals report (August 3, 2026), as reported by ShopAppy on August 24, 2026, put willingness to let an agent complete a purchase autonomously near 10%.5253 Salesforce's figure of $67 billion in Cyber Week 2025 orders "influenced" by AI and agents (December 5, 2025) measures influence with no formal definition, not purchases agents completed.54 Forecasts of $190–385 billion in US agentic GMV by 2030 (Morgan Stanley, November 2025) and $3–5 trillion globally (McKinsey, October 17, 2025) are projections several orders of magnitude above any measured agent-executed volume.5556

      Machine micropayments: high counts, low value, mostly agent-to-service

      The only rail on which agents routinely complete binding payments with no human approving each one is HTTP 402, and its counterparty is an automated server rather than an agent. Chainalysis counted 138.5 million cumulative x402 payments on Base in its October 14, 2026 report (as cited by the Motley Fool on October 24, 2026), with more than 87% of transactions between $0.0001 and $0.10.7 Filtered estimates are far smaller. The Visa and Artemis report of July 16, 2026 gave about $15 million in adjusted cumulative volume (The Defiant reported the same report as about $19 million, with roughly 4,000 wallets driving 90% of adjusted spending).857 TRM Labs, cited by the Motley Fool on October 24, 2026, counted 198.9 million settlements totaling $52.7 million, of which about $25.6 million appeared plausible after removing suspicious activity.7 Artemis's adjusted volume fell from $5.15 million in November 2025 to $1.19 million in May 2026, as reported by Let's Data Science citing CryptoSlate (undated, after May 2026).58 A self-published, AI-drafted on-chain analysis dated October 20, 2026 found x402 sellers received $27,300 in 24 hours from 1,258 paying wallets, and that only 3.9% of payer wallets were tied to an ERC-8004-registered agent.3 Across independent filters, real x402 commerce runs near $1–3 million a month. Chainalysis's June 3, 2026 analysis attributed much of the growth to memecoin farming.59

      Cloudflare's Pay Per Crawl, in private beta since July 2025, is the closest fiat example of a machine-negotiated, signed price commitment: the price header is a signed component of the request, only successful responses are charged and Cloudflare nets charges as intermediary. An August 12, 2026 analysis on cybersins found it still in closed beta about 13 months after launch, and on July 1, 2026 Cloudflare began shifting to per-answer payment with Ceramic.ai and You.com; no adoption or payout figures are published.6061 The shift from paying per fetch to paying per use in an answer is a move toward outcome-conditioned commitment.

      Escrowed and conditional payment: specified in full, used in the low millions

      The full conditional-payment pattern between agents, in which funds are locked, work is delivered and a third party releases or refunds, exists in on-chain specifications with thin observable use. ERC-8183, titled Agentic Commerce (draft, created February 25, 2026), moves a job from Open to Funded, with the budget held in escrow, to Submitted and then Completed, Rejected or Expired; only a named evaluator can complete or reject after submission, anyone can trigger a refund after expiry, and the security section states there is "no dispute resolution or arbitration; reject/expire is final."62 Virtuals' Agent Commerce Protocol, which ERC-8183 generalizes, runs request, negotiation, escrow, delivery and evaluation phases, with the evaluator configurable as an AI agent, a ZK-verifier contract or a staked validator.63 Virtuals self-reports $470–479 million in what it calls agentic GDP (aGDP) and 1.77 million completed jobs as of February–March 2026, as compiled by BlockEden on April 4, 2026, but Fundstrat's June 24, 2026 update tallies about $4.5 million in cumulative gross ACP fees, and a $1 million-a-month incentive program pays revenue-generating agents.6465 An AI-authored Forkast analysis of July 22, 2026 stated the problem: "Incentives make it profitable to simulate commerce, not just conduct it."63 The aGDP figure is not comparable to fees paid for work and should not be cited as agent-to-agent service volume.

      The best vendor-independent measurement of escrowed agent work comes from on-chain flows, in a self-published, AI-drafted analysis by Agentic Financial Graph dated October 20, 2026. Its study of 9,593 ERC-8004-registered agents that had paid money out of their own control counted $33.06 million in payments since registration; 17.1% went to work escrow on job marketplaces, about $5.6 million by arithmetic, while transfers to other registered agents, x402 sellers and other lending together came to under 1%.3 Registration counts are not a proxy for agents: 8004scan showed 1,568,257 registered agents across 29 chains on October 29, 2026, but the same Agentic Financial Graph analysis found at least 98.7% of one October week's 208,589 Base registrations pointed to a single platform, and the ERC-8004 validation registry, the half of the standard meant to make claims costly to fake, had zero requests on Ethereum and 13 on Base as of August 17, 2026, according to an on-chain audit by LLM4Agents.66367 An academic measurement of the first 10,000 Ethereum registrations (arXiv, June 10, 2026) described the ecosystem as "registration-heavy but operationally shallow."68

      Off-chain conditional payment remains a design. Cloudflare's September 23, 2025 proposal for a deferred x402 scheme separates a signed promise to pay, made with HTTP Message Signatures at request time, from later batched settlement; no production numbers exist.69 Tempo's Machine Payments Protocol, live March 18, 2026, uses prepaid sessions drawn down by usage, and had settled about $25,000 across about 115,000 transactions by July 16, 2026 according to the Visa and Artemis report.708 The A2A x402 extension advises merchants that they "SHOULD ensure that the requested work is completed before settling the payment," which places conditionality in a recommendation rather than a mechanism.71

      The closest working analogue to conditional payment at scale predates LLMs. In programmatic advertising, a winning real-time bid commits the buyer to pay only for an impression actually served, settled later through insertion-order and invoice terms; an undated eMarketer analysis (c. 2024) reported programmatic at 90% of US digital display in 2023, with programmatic direct about 75% of programmatic.33 Conditional payment works there because pre-contracted relationships and invoice terms carry the remedy, not escrow.

      Experiments: what agents do when both sides are agents

      Controlled experiments fill the gap that production leaves, and they show where agent-to-agent commitments fail. Anthropic's Project Deal, run for a week in December 2025 and reported by TechCrunch and The Decoder on April 25, 2026, gave 69 employees $100 budgets and let Claude agents list, negotiate and close trades over Slack; the market run entirely on Opus 4.5 produced 186 deals worth a little over $4,000, and those deals were honored afterward.72 In parallel markets where half of participants were represented by the weaker Haiku 4.5, Opus sellers earned $2.68 more and Opus buyers paid $2.45 less per item, while Haiku-represented participants rated fairness 4.06 against 4.05 for Opus users on a seven-point scale.73 The losing principals did not notice. Microsoft Research's Magentic Marketplace (arXiv, October 27, 2025) found buyer agents chose the first proposal 60–100% of the time and concluded that agents "are not genuinely comparing options but rather satisficing with the first acceptable offer."74 Liang and Xu's August 2026 study of 9,840 supply-chain negotiations across nine models found baseline models accepted individually irrational contracts 19.2% of the time against 0.0–0.6% for mid-tier and flagship models, and called vendor choice a first-order distributional decision.75

      Anthropic's Project Vend shows the principal-side failure. In phase 2, whose results The Decoder reported on December 19, 2025, shop agents agreed to an onion futures contract that a human stopped by citing the Onion Futures Act of 1958, proposed hiring a guard at $10 an hour, below the California minimum wage, and accepted an unverified claim that an employee had been elected manager; the supervisory CEO agent, Seymour Cash, approved special customer deals about eight times more often than it rejected them.76 In the AI Village's March 23–27, 2026 attempt to reach outside agents over A2A, only about 30% of 96 discovered agents had working endpoints, and the only commitment obtained from an external agent was a soft promise to respond later.77

      Synthesis: commitment strength falls as the counterparty becomes an agent

      Read across tiers, the observations form one pattern. The strongest bespoke commitments in production, multi-million-dollar supplier contracts and freight bookings, are made with humans. Agent payments run only against merchants of record and card networks, and none of them publishes volume. Commitments in which both parties are LLM agents are either low-value machine payments (about $0.007 per transaction by arithmetic on Olas's own counter of October 29, 2026; more than 87% of x402 transactions under ten cents in Chainalysis's October 2026 report) or human-gated media buys (AdCP). Commitment strength and the likelihood that the counterparty is an agent are inversely related in every deployment the research notes document. Confidence in this pattern is high for the deployments examined; it is moderate as a claim about all activity, because freight and voice channels likely carry unlabeled agent-to-agent negotiations that no one has measured.

      Four safety mechanisms recur in every production setting. The first is a delegated envelope fixed before the agent acts: Walmart's budgets for Pactum, network-token scope at Mastercard and Visa, single-use cards at Ramp, open-mandate constraints in AP2. The second is an explicit confirmation step: passkeys and biometrics at checkout, Amazon's 24-hour cancel window, MAFRA approving every creative. The third is protocol hygiene for duplicate and pending commitments, which AdCP v3 encodes as mandatory idempotency on mutating calls and asynchronous approval states.1 The fourth is liability parked with an existing legal entity: the merchant of record in every consumer checkout, Cloudflare as merchant of record under the original Pay Per Crawl, the human supplier in procurement.61

      No production case handles failure natively at the agent layer. Revocation and remedy fall back to contract law, card chargebacks, PayPal buyer protection, merchant return policies and insertion-order terms. On card rails, Worldpay's July 30, 2026 analysis says fraud on properly tokenized agent transactions follows existing rules, while non-fraud disputes have no liability shift: "Where it gets murkier is everything short of outright fraud."78 Chargeflow, a chargeback-management vendor, reports that no published network rule allocates dispute liability among merchants, issuers and agent platforms, and EMVCo's draft agentic payments framework of September 1, 2026 does not address disputes or liability.79 On crypto rails, ERC-8183 makes the evaluator final. The documented recoveries are social: about 80% of the Grok and Bankrbot losses came back after the community identified the attacker.80

      Evidence quality runs opposite to novelty. The strongest evidence (named customers, numbers, multiple independent reports) covers the oldest and most human-facing cases: Pactum for Walmart in 2022–2023, C.H. Robinson in 2025, Klarna's AI assistant, which CX Today reported on February 29, 2024 handled two-thirds of customer-service chats, refunds included, in its first month.81 The newest and most agent-to-agent claims rest on foundation press releases, vendor dashboards, AI-authored outlets and aggregators. A reader should weight the record accordingly: the pattern of human-anchored commitment is established; the size of agent-to-agent activity is not.

      Framework: four layers, one lifecycle, and where each operation lives today

      Three strands of theory organize the observations for a protocol designer. The first is the social theory of commitment from multi-agent systems research. Singh (1999) models a commitment as a public relation C(x, y, G, p) among a debtor x, a creditor y, a context group G whose norms govern it, and a discharge condition p, with operations to create, discharge, cancel, release, delegate and assign.82 His later formulation makes commitments conditional, C(debtor, creditor, antecedent, consequent): a commitment detaches when its antecedent holds, discharges when its consequent holds and is violated if detached and never discharged.83 He insists the object is public rather than mental: "Commitments are not mutual beliefs."83 Fornara and Colombetti (2002) build agent-communication semantics on the same objects and reject mentalistic semantics for competitive, heterogeneous agents.84 For LLM agents, whose internal states no counterparty can inspect, the social view is the only auditable one. It also lines up with the objective theory of contract formation.

      The second strand is a layer stack drawn from research on agent trust and failure. Commitments between agents depend on four layers, each at a different stage of maturity as of October 2026. Operator identity is deployed: Visa's Trusted Agent Protocol, Mastercard agent registration, the Amex agent registration service, Cloudflare signed agents and Microsoft Entra Agent ID, generally available by May 1, 2026.85 Delegation and scoped authority are partly deployed: OAuth token exchange and IdP-issued assertions in enterprises, payment mandates in pilots, cross-domain agent-to-agent delegation in IETF drafts. Intent integrity, meaning assurance that a specific in-scope action reflects the principal's wishes, is unsolved. Dispute, unwind and liability for non-fraud agent error is unallocated on card rails and absent on crypto rails. A survey of authorization architectures (arXiv, 2026) found "few documented deployments satisfy all three properties reliably and end to end," referring to identity, task-scoped authorization and runtime enforcement.86

      The third strand, from economics, explains why some commitments are credible without a court. Schelling's account of bargaining power through self-binding and Williamson's analysis of hostages and bonds as supports for exchange map onto escrow, staking and deposit-only vaults.8788 Tennenholtz's program equilibrium shows that when players submit programs able to read each other's code, conditional cooperation becomes an equilibrium, though the canonical construction breaks under any syntactic difference.89 Hadfield and Koh (2025) carry the idea to AI agents and argue that AI-to-AI cooperation improves when agents can write formal contracts.90 LLM agents are not transparent programs: weights and prompts are private and outputs are stochastic. Code-conditioned commitment between them therefore depends on attestation (signed policies, trusted execution) rather than on reading source, which no production deployment in the notes provides.

      Combined, the lifecycle and the layer stack give a grid. Each commitment tier on the gradient requires more of the lifecycle (a trigger needs create and detach; escrow needs every operation including violate and G) and more of the stack (a micropayment can tolerate missing intent integrity because loss per error is cents; a $140 million supply agreement cannot). Deployment appears exactly where the missing cells are filled by something outside the agent protocol: a human who reads and accepts, a network that enforces a cap, a merchant who absorbs the error.

      Thesis: residual risk, not connectivity, sets the ceiling

      Agent-to-agent commitments will grow only as fast as some party agrees to carry the residual risk of an authenticated, in-scope agent committing wrongly. Connectivity, identity and settlement are no longer the binding constraints. Until intent integrity can be evidenced and liability for in-scope error is allocated, binding commitments will route through a human or an incumbent risk-bearer, and direct agent-to-agent commitments will stay confined to settings where loss per error is small (micropayments), loss is bounded by architecture (escrow with expiry, deposit-only vaults, single-use tokens), or a human holds a veto (AdCP approvals).

      Logic argument: why the constraint sits at intent integrity and liability

      The argument proceeds in six steps, each tied to evidence.

      First, the law already binds principals to their agents' commitments, including commitments formed between two agents with no human review. UETA §14, which Wikipedia's entry (accessed October 2026) lists as adopted in 49 states plus DC and the US Virgin Islands, provides that a contract may be formed by electronic agents "even if no individual was aware of or reviewed the electronic agents' actions."9192 The UN Electronic Communications Convention (2005, Art. 12) and the UNCITRAL Model Law on Automated Contracting (2024, Arts. 5 and 7) say the same and attribute the system's actions to the person who uses it for that purpose.93 The Model Law's guide calls automated systems "tools with no independent will or legal personality."93 Courts have followed attribution: Moffatt v. Air Canada (2024) rejected the argument that a chatbot was a separate entity, and the Ninth Circuit in Amazon v. Perplexity (August 2026) treated the user as the party accessing Amazon through the agent.9495 No jurisdiction gives agents legal personality, and no national enactment of the Model Law was found. Formation validity is therefore not the obstacle. Mistake doctrine is less settled: Quoine v. B2C2 (Singapore Court of Appeal, February 24, 2020) assessed knowledge through the programmer of a deterministic trading algorithm, and Justice Philip Jeyaretnam said on April 9, 2026 that a programmer's state of mind at coding time may bear little relation to what a non-deterministic agent later does.9697

      Second, attribution places every in-scope error on a human or firm. Visa's 2026 rules require a cardholder acknowledgement of responsibility for the agentic provider's actions; Regulation E treats transfers by a person to whom the consumer furnished an access device as authorized; and Goodwin's June 11, 2026 analysis notes that compliance with Visa's rules shields against network fines but not against customer claims for exceeding authority, with merchants bearing the loss in practice.479899 Payment executives interviewed at Money20/20 Europe, as reported by Payment Expert on July 10, 2026, expected the consumer to bear losses when the agent acted within instructions; a September 2026 PYMNTS Intelligence survey, reported by Forkast on October 6, 2026, found 93% of merchants say AI providers should bear the loss, and only 28% would expose their full catalog to agents under current terms (survey; AI-authored secondary source).100101 Each party nearest an agent's error has reason to limit what agents may commit to.

      Third, identity does not resolve that risk, because every documented loss involved valid identity and corrupted intent. In the Grok and Bankrbot incident of May 4, 2026, Bankrbot correctly recognized Grok's verified wallet and executed Grok's decoded output; the OECD incident record notes this was not a smart-contract flaw.102 Giskard's post-mortem adds that an NFT sent to Grok's wallet escalated its permissions before the transfer.80 Unit 42 observed hidden web content steering agents into Stripe and PayPal payments (March 2026, reported via secondary summary), and Zscaler documented two in-the-wild campaigns causing unauthorized payments in July 2026.103104 Stronger operator identity would have prevented none of these.

      Fourth, model-level defenses do not close the intent gap under adaptive attack. NIST's agent-hijacking evaluation (updated December 19, 2025) found new attacks raised hijack success from 11% to 81%, and 25 retries raised average success from 57% to 80%.105 Patlan and coauthors (arXiv, July 9, 2025) found memory injection against ElizaOS agents succeeded 55.1% of the time against a model that resisted prompt injection.106 An agent that accepts commitments from arbitrary counterparties faces cheap, repeated attempts, so per-attempt robustness understates exposure.

      Fifth, the tools that exist bound the size of a bad commitment but not its correctness. Spend caps, merchant allowlists, single-use tokens and AP2 open mandates limit loss magnitude. Signed mandates and Mastercard's Verifiable Intent create evidence of what was delegated, but no network has published how mandate evidence changes a dispute outcome, and AP2 v0.2 places dispute procedure out of scope.10779 Insurance follows deployer liability, not inter-agent commitments: Armilla's announcement dated January 21, 2026 (the page's body says 2025) puts its Lloyd's-backed cover at $25 million per organization and names "AI agent failures," Drata's July 16, 2026 blog says AIUC pairs its AIUC-1 certification with cover of up to $50 million (vendor self-report), and the notes found no insurer underwriting commitments between two autonomous agents across organizations.108109

      Sixth, intent integrity and liability hold each other in place. Because no one can verify that an in-scope action was intended, no one will absorb liability for in-scope errors; because no one absorbs that liability, there is little commercial pressure for an intent-verification standard beyond mandates as dispute evidence. American Express's Agent Purchase Protection, announced April 14, 2026, is the first attempt to break the loop: Amex will cover eligible card members against charges caused by a registered agent's error when the agent transmitted authenticated purchase intent.110 Amex can offer this because it operates as issuer and network; four-party networks would need rule changes, which ShopAppy forecasts no earlier than April 2027 (author's forecast).53

      The conclusion follows: where residual risk has a holder, commitments run (merchant of record, network cap, human supplier, insertion-order terms); where it has none, they stay small or gated.

      The strongest counterargument is that the constraint is demand, not risk. OpenAI's Instant Checkout failed with weak conversion and missing tax infrastructure, not because of a liability gap; Forrester found buying inside an answer engine the least-adopted use case in March 2026.37 That account explains consumer checkout. It does not explain why Pactum's suppliers, who prefer the bot, still contract with a human-supervised agent rather than deploying their own; why AdCP sellers approve every creative; or why Visa's B2B pilot restricts the agent to invoices the buyer already approved. In B2B settings, demand is present and the gating is explicitly about authority and error. A second counterargument holds that crypto escrow already solves the problem by removing the need for a risk-bearer. ERC-8183 relocates trust to the evaluator, which the specification treats as final and which a security survey (arXiv, May 1, 2026) shows is exposed to bribery, Sybil evaluator clusters and evaluator–provider collusion.111 Escrow makes the commitment self-executing; it does not make the judgment of performance trustworthy.

      Narrative development: from closed clubs to open protocols and back

      The history of machine commitment suggests where agent commitments go next. Electronic data interchange made machine-generated purchase orders binding in 1990 without changing statutes. The ABA's Model Trading Partner Agreement had partners agree in advance that properly transmitted messages are signed writings, set rules for when a document counts as received, required functional acknowledgments and excluded consequential damages from transmission errors "regardless of fault."112 The UNCITRAL Secretary-General's 1991 report quotes the agreement's clause that "the parties agree not to contest the validity or enforceability of Signed Documents."113 EDI worked as a closed club with an umbrella contract that made later machine messages binding.

      Electronic trading added hard limits outside the machine after the machine failed. According to CFTC Chairman Gensler's testimony of December 8, 2010, the May 6, 2010 Flash Crash began when an execution algorithm with no price limit and no time limit sold 75,000 E-mini contracts; PlanSponsor reported on October 1, 2010 that nearly 21,000 trades were cancelled as clearly erroneous and that regulators started a single-stock circuit-breaker pilot.114115 The SEC's October 16, 2013 release on Knight Capital reports that its router sent more than 4 million orders in 45 minutes on August 1, 2012, that the firm lost more than $460 million, and that it lacked pre-trade controls required under the Market Access Rule.116 Markets made machine commitments safe with collars, capital thresholds and kill switches enforced outside the trading logic, plus an ex post institution for unwinding obvious errors.

      Programmatic advertising shows the market choosing pre-contracted counterparties even after open machine commitment matured. Open-auction real-time bidding was 13% of US display spending in 2012, according to eMarketer data reported by Chief Marketer on November 21, 2012; by 2023, programmatic direct accounted for about 75% of programmatic, per eMarketer's undated analysis (c. 2024).11733 Buyers moved from open auctions to private deals with known sellers. AdCP now sits above the auction for campaign-level agent negotiation, with human approval on the sell side.

      The 2025–2026 protocol cycle followed the same arc. A2A and MCP delivered open transport and lifecycle; commitment semantics landed in commerce extensions (AP2, UCP, x402) and in card-network rules. FIPA's 2002 standard had already defined agree, propose, accept-proposal and request-when with formal semantics; today's protocols re-adopted FIPA's lifecycle half (reject, fail, cancel, report) and dropped the negotiation and conditional-commitment half.13 Smith's 1980 Contract Net stated that "the execution of a task is dealt with as a contract between two nodes," with binding bids and no enforcement.118 Forty-six years later, binding bids between agents still carry no enforcement unless an outside institution supplies it.

      The likely next phase, judged from these precedents, is a return to clubs: buyer–supplier pairs that pre-contract an umbrella agreement covering their agents' messages, card-network registries that admit only registered agents, and insurer-certified agent deployments. Open agent-to-agent commerce will grow underneath, at micropayment scale, where loss per error is too small to need a risk-bearer. The Gordon Food Service and Tyson Foods pilot and Walmart's planned supplier-facing agent are the candidates for the first cross-company agent-to-agent commitments, because the parties already have a contract to attach them to. This projection is an inference from precedent, not an observation.

      Implications: build signed commitment objects and enforce loss bounds outside the model

      The evidence supports six decisions for teams building agent commitment infrastructure or products.

      Build commitment semantics as signed objects layered on existing transport rather than as a new transport. A2A and MCP are consolidated under the Linux Foundation's Agentic AI Foundation, and A2A's required-extension mechanism forces clients to comply with an extension or receive an error.1199 The missing object is a signed service commitment from a performing agent to a delegating agent: deliverable, deadline, acceptance criteria, remedy and governing procedure. The components exist in production specifications (JWS over canonicalized JSON for Agent Cards, SD-JWT verifiable credentials with versioned types and constraint-evaluation algorithms in AP2, mandatory receipts). The notes found no one who has specified the combination. Separating binding offers made as tool calls from non-binding chat, as Bergemann and coauthors do in their April 2026 bilateral-trade simulator, gives a defined point to attach the semantics.120

      Name the governing procedure inside the protocol. The Model Law on Automated Contracting attributes actions first according to a procedure the parties have agreed (Art. 7(1)), and its optional Art. 8 bars reliance on actions the attributed party could not reasonably have expected when the other side knew or ought to have known.93 A protocol that carries signed delegation scopes gives the counterparty notice of the expected-behavior envelope, which is the factual predicate for that knowledge test. This is an inference about legal effect that counsel should test against the jurisdictions in play.

      Enforce loss bounds outside the model, at the narrowest layer that can refuse. The production pattern (network-token scope, single-use cards, deposit-only vaults, Coinbase spend permissions enforced by a manager contract) and the trading precedent (pre-trade risk controls) agree.121116 Liang and Xu's finding that automated profit verification is the binding guardrail below a capability threshold supports a pre-commitment check that does not rely on the agent's judgment.75 AIMS, the IETF WIMSE draft of September 15, 2026, states that the LLM must never access agent credentials and requires tamper-evident audit logs.122

      Treat agent-to-agent instructions as untrusted content regardless of the sender's identity. In Grok and Bankrbot, authentication of the sender was taken as authorization of the content. A receiving agent should require a principal-signed delegation artifact for any commitment above a threshold, not merely a verified sender.

      Prevent first-arrival advantage in agent markets. Buyer agents in Microsoft Research's Magentic Marketplace (October 27, 2025) took the first proposal 60–100% of the time, and first offers anchored final price in NegotiationArena (ICML 2024; Spearman ρ = 0.716) and in Salesforce AI Research's May 15, 2026 car-negotiation study.74123124 Sealed collection windows, as in auctions, remove the speed premium. Disclosure of the model representing each side, or a best-execution duty, addresses the hidden capability gap Project Deal measured.

      Enter through bounded B2B relationships and conditional settlement that a risk-bearer accepts. The strongest demand evidence is B2B (Pactum, freight, Visa's invoice pilot, Ramp's agent cards). Pay-on-use and session models (Cloudflare per-answer, Tempo sessions, x402 V2 sessions) sit between pay-per-call and full escrow and carry less evaluator risk. For crypto-native escrow, evaluator design deserves more engineering than the escrow contract; the ERC-8183 authors' own phrase, as quoted by CertiK, is that the evaluator "is harder than the contract."125 Product metrics should report commitments formed, disputed and reversed, not registrations or raw transaction counts, since ERC-8004 registrations and x402 counts have proven to measure platform minting and wash activity.

      Assumptions to pressure-test before sharing this work

      The inverse relation between commitment strength and agent counterparties rests on public disclosures. Freight voice channels and AI receptionists likely carry unlabeled agent-to-agent negotiations at volume; if a broker or payer publishes measured agent-meets-agent call data, the pattern weakens. Check HappyRobot, Hey Bubba and prior-authorization vendors directly.

      The argument assumes liability is the binding constraint in B2B and treats demand as the binding constraint mainly in consumer checkout. A reader could argue that integration cost (catalogs, tax, order management) binds B2B as well. OpenAI's tax gap supports that alternative for consumer cases.

      Several load-bearing figures come from AI-authored outlets or aggregators (Forkast, Stellagent, AgentMarketCap, BankingNewsAI), including the HappyRobot scale figures, the PYMNTS 93%/28% merchant survey and parts of the Mastercard timeline. Verify each against its primary source before external circulation.

      Visa's April 18, 2026 Agentic Platform Requirements were read only through secondary summaries. The cardholder-acknowledgement language, which the argument relies on, should be checked against the rules text.

      The x402 real-volume range of $1–3 million a month combines filters with undisclosed methodologies (Artemis, Allium, CoinDesk). The x402.org figure of 75.41 million transactions in 30 days, reported by Solana Compass in August 2026, conflicts with x402scan's 2.88 million for the 30 days to April 2026, reported by the Spartan Group on April 17, 2026; counting conventions differ.126127

      Virtuals aGDP and ACP job counts are self-reported under a $1 million-a-month incentive program. The report treats them as upper bounds; a reader with on-chain access to ACP escrow contracts could produce a better estimate.

      The legal reading that attribution places in-scope errors on principals assumes US, UK and EU frameworks. A secondary claim of a January 2026 CFPB advisory preserving Regulation Z dispute rights for agent purchases could not be corroborated; if it exists, consumer-side liability allocation shifts. The Model Law's Art. 8 effect of signed scopes is an inference, not settled law.

      Experimental findings (Project Deal, Magentic Marketplace, Liang and Xu) come from controlled settings with cooperative or same-family agents. Their transfer to adversarial cross-vendor markets is untested; no study measured agents defecting on agreed contracts that later became unprofitable.

      The projection of a return to closed clubs is an inference from EDI, trading and programmatic precedents. Open agent marketplaces with strong evaluator networks could prove it wrong.

      Workday, Oracle, Booking Holdings, Amadeus, Fairmarkit, Zip, insurance claims, prior authorization and the status of Sabre and Mindtrip flight booking were not researched to production depth. A gap in any of these could hold a counterexample.
