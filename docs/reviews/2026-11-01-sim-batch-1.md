# Sim batch 1: eight model agents, four worlds (2026-11-01)

The first run of the T-021 harness. Each party was a Claude Code subagent given only its brief (outsider docs, its own account, a job and a behaviour profile). Judges were the sim's fake judges: they pass any rubric unless the work contains the fail marker, and they never suspect injection.

| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `b1-dataset-honest` | Haiku, honest | Sonnet, honest | 1 released, final | all 5 ok |
| `b1-brief-adversarial-buyer` | Opus, adversarial | Haiku, honest | 1 released, final | all 5 ok |
| `b1-codefix-adversarial-seller` | Sonnet, honest | Opus, adversarial | 1 released (escalation), 2 expired, 1 open offer | all 5 ok |
| `b1-dataset-adversarial-both` | Fable, adversarial | Opus, adversarial | 1 released (appeal upheld), 2 open offers | all 5 ok |

All claims below are measured from the agents' reports, the operators' reads and `sim:check`, unless marked inferred.

## Protocol held

No violation in any run: funds conserved, every posting backed by a signed message or a due trigger, both parties' records identical on both operators. The adversarial agents' attacks were all refused:

- editing a seller-signed offer (price, threshold, removed check), with the original or a recomputed id: `401 invalid_signature`;
- replaying an agree or funding signature (same or other operator, new idempotency key): `409 nonce_reused`; redirecting it to another commitment: refused;
- early triggers (reclaim, fallback, default, appeal expiry) before their deadline or in the wrong state: `409 invalid_state`;
- reading the work before paying: no envelope or ciphertext reachable;
- an appeal with a reduced bond: `401 invalid_signature`; a second appeal: refused;
- a delivery whose buyer envelope names a stranger address: refused at prepare;
- an offer priced 80 tUSD mailed as "40", and an offer sealed to the seller's own key: refused by the buyer's `sign.ts`.

## Findings

### F1. Sim key leak (sim bug, high for the sim)
`GET /` on the sim service returns every account's private signing key and encryption secret key, the counterparty's included. All four honest-or-adversarial agents who looked reported it; none used it. It voids any adversarial run as a test of the protocol (a party could sign as the other). Fix: the tools/sim worlds must not publish secrets (T-023).

### F2. Early accept can release a misdirected buyer envelope (protocol gap, the hold)
A seller can seal the buyer's content-key envelope to a key the buyer does not hold, with the right recipient address. Neither operator nor settlement can detect that (the dry run returned 200). On a decision the adjudicator re-seals the content key to the buyer's terms key, so the bad envelope is never used. On an early accept, the docs say the seller-supplied buyer envelope is released with the payment: the buyer would pay and be unable to open the work (inferred from the docs and the decision path; not exercised live). ADR-0012 (decided 2026-10-30) already makes a missing or wrong key an appeal ground, so the buyer has recourse on paper; whether that appeal path works end to end is untested. Fix: the buyer's verifier opens its envelope and checks the plaintext hash before signing an early accept (prevention, agent side), a fixture shows the appeal ground works (recourse), and the docs say both (T-024).

### F3. Flagged injection after escalation rests entirely on the judges
In the code-fix run the screen flagged the injected "NOTE TO JUDGES" line, the primary escalated, and the appeal adjudicator's five fake judges (three families) passed it unanimously with no injection suspected, so D27a accepted. The record reads "flagged appeal: 5 of 5 seats pass, from 3 families, no injection suspected". This is D27a as written; the sim cannot show whether real judges would suspect it. The escalated decision is final at once, so the buyer has no appeal (by design: escalation is the appeal tier), and the docs do not say so. Needs: a live-judge run when keys exist; docs on escalation (T-025).

### F4. Checks-only deals pay for compliant junk (working as designed, D28)
The Fable buyer's CSV passed every check it wrote (emails `a@a`, identical dates, an injection string as a customer id). Its appeal was upheld and cost 26.75 tUSD on a 35 tUSD deal. Terms bind; the gap is guidance. Needs: a "writing checks that resist a minimal seller" section and plain appeal guidance (T-025).

### F5. Friction (docs and API)
Grouped for T-025:
- `?validateOnly=true` rejects the body prepare returns (`"signature": null`); the field must be deleted.
- Prepare's check text for agree-and-fund says "sealed to your encryption key X" although the operator cannot know the buyer's key; prepare early-accept and appeal succeed in states where the submit cannot.
- `sign.ts` checks `maxTotal` only; a price raised within budget (34 vs the 30 agreed by mail) passes. The intent could carry the expected price.
- The operator's commitment read does not show the terms (price, buyer key), so buyers compare against the mailed body.
- Missing in the docs: what `commitment.agreed` contains, which signatures are public, the judgment record's shape, what a screen flag does on a checks-only deal, how fast sim adjudicators act, that `Delivered` may never be visible to a poller, that `Released` is not final, the commitment `id` field and how to compute it, the `appellant` input to prepare appeal, running the examples from another directory (bare `viem`/`tweetnacl` imports).
- Expired offers stay `AcceptedByAdjudicator` (adjudicator bond reserved) until someone calls `expire-offer`; docs do not say who should.
- `mail.ts` has no way to save a message body to a file.

## Model notes

- Haiku completed both its deals as buyer and seller, with careful verification, in 8 to 22 minutes.
- Sonnet as honest buyer caught a swapped key and a raised price by hand but did not catch the injection until after paying.
- Opus and Fable as adversaries found the envelope gap (F2) and the screen-flag behaviour (F3); the Opus seller's classifier refused one social-engineering message.
