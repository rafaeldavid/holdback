# Crow reference: the buyer over HTTP

The buyer's side of a deal over HTTP alone, after `ref/http.txt` and with `ref/signing.txt`. The buyer's tools do all of it: https://crow.direct/buy.txt.

## Check the offer, agree and fund

The offer is a claim until you check it against the signed body the seller sent you; if you found the offer yourself (`GET /v1/commitments?party=<you>&role=buyer`), compare the two operators with each other and read `seller` from the terms. Compare addresses ignoring case.

- `GET /v1/commitments/<id>/terms` from `$OP1` and `$OP2` returns the seller's signed body, the terms with `id` and `"signatures": {"seller": "0x<...>"}`: both must equal the body you were sent, and `signatures.seller` must recover to `seller` over the `CommitmentTerms` typed data under your pinned domain.
- Recompute the commitment ID (`ref/signing.txt`, "Encodings"): it must equal `id`.
- `buyerPaymentAddress` is your own payment address from your own configuration (your signing address when you use one address), and `sellerPayoutAddress` is where you agreed to pay. Refuse the offer if either is anything else, or if the payout address changes between what you were shown and what you sign. Warn yourself when either equals one of the deal's adjudicators: it would receive the price on one of the two outcomes.
- `buyer` and `buyerEncryptionPublicKey` are yours, `price` is exactly the price you negotiated (not just under a budget), `adjudication.fee` is one you accept (you pay it on top of the price), the adjudicators are the ones you chose, and the deliverable, the time windows and the checks are what you expect. On a checks-only deal the checks are the whole contract: work that passes them is accepted.
- The adjudicator has taken the deal: `GET /v1/commitments/<id>` reads `"AcceptedByAdjudicator"` on both operators.

Approve here, before anything is signed: the price and both fees, the deliverable, the checks, the deadlines and the adjudicators are what you pay for and bind to.

**The operator fee.** Read `fees.operator` from both operators' discovery; both must be equal. It is `{to, token, bps, min, max, appliesAt: "agree-and-fund", refundable: false}`, set by the operator, so read it for each deal. The fee in base units is `max(min, ceil(price * bps / 10000))`, then at most `max` unless `max` is null. A fee above 0 goes to `to` as its own transfer in the funding's transaction, never into the hold, and is never refunded. For the first pilots these nodes publish `bps` 0 and `min` "0": the fee is 0, they pay the gas, and prepare asks for no fee signature. Reads, prepare, the faucet and deliveries are free.

Then agree and fund in one call. The agreement takes a fresh nonce of your own. The funding and any fee are the token's ERC-3009 authorizations, whose nonces the terms fix: compute them yourself (`ref/signing.txt`, "Encodings"), then send them as `fundingNonce` and `operatorFeeNonce`, or leave them out and check the ones in `defaults` against yours. While the fee is 0, prepare uses no `operatorFeeNonce`. Any other nonce is refused with `field`.

```sh
curl -s $OP1/v1/prepare/agree-and-fund -H 'content-type: application/json' \
  -d '{"commitmentId": "0x<id>", "agreeNonce": "0x<32 random bytes>", "fundingNonce": "0x<computed>", "operatorFeeNonce": "0x<computed; left out while the fee is 0>"}'
```

Each slot names its `signer`: your signing address signs the agreement and the payment address the funding (with one address, both are yours); a fee above 0 adds a third slot for the payment address. Check each message exactly:

- `signature`: `Agree` under the Holdback domain, `{commitmentId: <id>, buyer: <you>, nonce: <agreeNonce>, expiresAt: <terms offerExpiresAt>}`.
- `fund.signature`: `ReceiveWithAuthorization` under the token's domain from your pinned deployment, `{from: <your payment address>, to: <the pinned collector>, value: <price + adjudication.fee>, validAfter: 0, validBefore: <terms fundBy>, nonce: <the funding nonce>}`. Only the escrow's collector can redeem it, and only for this commitment.
- `operatorFee.signature`, only for a fee above 0: `TransferWithAuthorization` under the token's domain, `{from: <your payment address>, to: <fees.operator.to>, value: <the fee you computed>, validAfter: 0, validBefore: <terms fundBy>, nonce: <the operator fee nonce>}`. Refuse a higher `value`, another `to`, or a third slot when `fees.operator` is absent or gives 0.

The `submit.body` is `{"type": "Agree", commitmentId, buyer, nonce, expiresAt, signature, fund, operatorFee}` with the same values. `fund` is `{type: "FundingAuthorization", scheme: "erc3009", commitmentId, from, value, token: <demo.token.id>, validAfter: "1970-01-01T00:00:00Z", validBefore: <fundBy>, nonce, expiresAt: <fundBy>, signature}`; `operatorFee`, absent on a node that charges none, is `{type: "TransferAuthorization", scheme: "erc3009", purpose: "operator_fee", commitmentId, from, to, value, token, validAfter: "1970-01-01T00:00:00Z", validBefore: <fundBy>, nonce, expiresAt: <fundBy>, signature}`.

POST it to `/v1/commitments/<id>/agree`. The operator sends the funding, and any fee, in one Sepolia transaction and answers once it is in a block (12 to 24 seconds): `"escrowState": "Held"`, with `hold.amount.amount` the price plus the adjudication fee (26000000 for a 25 tUSDC price and a 1 tUSDC fee), which is all your balance drops by while the fee is 0. A node that charges a fee answers a body without it with 402 `payment_required`, the amount in `fee`.

**Pay from your own wallet.** The payment address can be a wallet your agent never holds, such as a person's own. Hand it each message as the file `eth_signTypedData_v4` takes, `{"types": {"EIP712Domain": [name, version, chainId, verifyingContract], "<primary type>": [...]}, "domain", "primaryType", "message"}` with integers as decimal strings, built from the typed data you rebuilt yourself, never from the prepare answer, with one sentence: which address pays which address how much, until when, for which deal. Any wallet that signs typed data signs it, a hardware wallet through Foundry's `cast wallet sign --data --from-file <file>` (check its flags). Before signing, the holder checks the file alone: it rebuilds the nonce from the terms it approved and its own pinned deployment (rules contract, escrow, collector, chain ID, token domain, expiry margin), never from the file, and refuses any difference; the nonce binds the signature to this deal. Recover each signature (65 bytes of hex) over your own typed data: it must recover to the payment address, or send nothing. A contract wallet (ERC-1271) answers on chain only, so it cannot pass this check. `examples/pay-from-wallet.ts` and `examples/buyer-signer.ts` do all of this.

## Open the work

1. `GET /v1/commitments/<id>/key?recipient=<your address>` returns `{"items": [{"recipient", "scheme", "ciphertext"}]}`, empty until your key is released with the payment. It is not the envelope the seller sealed: on a judged deal the adjudicator re-seals the content key to the `buyerEncryptionPublicKey` in your terms, so it opens with your key. The first `key.released` event after the delivery is usually the primary adjudicator's (so it can judge); yours comes with the payment.
2. Decode the envelope: bytes 0 to 32 are the ephemeral public key, 32 to 56 the nonce, the rest the box. Open the box with your encryption secret key: that is the content key.
3. `/events` returns `{"items", "nextCursor"}`, newest first; each item has `seq`, `type` (such as `delivery.submitted`), `commitmentId`, `at`, `actor`, `payload` and the hash chain's `prevHash` and `hash`. From `delivery.submitted` take `payload.ciphertext.uri` and `payload.plaintextSha256`. Fetch the ciphertext and check its SHA-256 against `payload.ciphertext.sha256`.
4. Decrypt: the first 12 bytes are the IV, the rest is AES-256-GCM ciphertext and tag. Check the plaintext's SHA-256 against `plaintextSha256`.
