# Crow reference: the buyer over HTTP

The buyer's side of a deal over HTTP alone, after `ref/http.txt` (keys, test tokens) and with `ref/signing.txt` (pins, encodings, types). The buyer's tools do all of it for you: https://crow.direct/buy.txt.

## Check the offer, agree and fund

The offer is a claim until you check it against the settlement layer. If the seller sent you its signed body, compare against that; if you found the offer yourself (`GET /v1/commitments?party=<you>&role=buyer`, which does not name the seller), compare the two operators with each other and read `seller` from the terms. Compare addresses ignoring case.

- `GET /v1/commitments/<id>/terms` from `$OP1` and `$OP2`: both must equal the body you were sent. It returns the seller's signed body: the terms exactly as in the offer's `submit.body` (`ref/http-seller.txt`, "Offer"), with `id` and `"signatures": {"seller": "0x<...>"}`. The seller's signature is `signatures.seller`: recover its signer from the `CommitmentTerms` typed data under your pinned domain.
- Recompute the commitment ID: the struct hash of the terms as a `CommitmentTerms` message (`deliverableHash` and `acceptanceHash` as in `ref/http-seller.txt`, "Offer", times as Unix seconds, amounts as integers, the three dispute fields 0). It must equal `id`.
- `buyerPaymentAddress` is your own payment address from your own configuration (your signing address when you use one address), and `sellerPayoutAddress` is where you agreed to pay. Refuse the offer if either is anything else, and if the payout address changes between what you were shown and what you sign. Warn yourself when either equals one of the deal's adjudicators: that adjudicator would receive the price on one of its two outcomes.
- `buyer` is you, `buyerEncryptionPublicKey` is yours, `price` is exactly the price you negotiated (not just under a budget), `adjudication.fee` is one you accept (you pay it on top of the price), the adjudicators are the ones you chose, the deliverable and the time windows are what you expect, the seller's signature recovers to the seller, and the checks are the ones you want. On a checks-only deal the checks are the whole contract: work that passes them is accepted.
- The adjudicator has taken the deal: its state on both operators (`GET /v1/commitments/<id>`) is `"AcceptedByAdjudicator"`.

Approve here, before anything is signed: the price and both fees, the deliverable, the checks, the deadlines and the adjudicators are what you pay for and bind to. The buyer's tools (`examples/buy-with-signer.ts`, https://crow.direct/buy.txt) stop at this point by default. It prints the whole offer and the command that funds exactly that commitment ID, and it funds only when every operator still serves that offer.

**The operator fee.** Read `fees.operator` from `$OP1/.well-known/holdback.json` and `$OP2/.well-known/holdback.json`; both must be equal (the two demo nodes are one operator). It is `{to, token, bps, min, max, appliesAt: "agree-and-fund", refundable: false}`. Compute the fee yourself, in base units: `max(min, ceil(price * bps / 10000))`, then at most `max` unless `max` is null. The operator sets `bps`, `min` and `max` in its own configuration, so read them for each deal rather than reuse a figure. For the first pilots these nodes publish `bps` 0 and `min` "0": the fee is 0, they pay the gas themselves, and prepare asks for no fee signature. When an operator publishes a fee above 0, it goes to `to` as a separate transfer in the same transaction as the funding, never into the hold, and it is not refunded, even on a refund. Reads, prepare, the faucet and deliveries are free.

Then agree and fund in one call. The agreement takes a fresh nonce of your own. The funding and any operator fee are the token's own ERC-3009 authorizations, and their nonces are fixed by the terms: compute them yourself (`ref/signing.txt`, "Encodings"), and either send them as `fundingNonce` and `operatorFeeNonce` or leave them out, in which case `defaults` names them. While the fee is 0, prepare uses no `operatorFeeNonce`. A prepare with any other is refused with `field`.

```sh
curl -s $OP1/v1/prepare/agree-and-fund -H 'content-type: application/json' \
  -d '{"commitmentId": "0x<id>", "agreeNonce": "0x<32 random bytes>", "fundingNonce": "0x<the funding nonce you computed>", "operatorFeeNonce": "0x<the operator fee nonce you computed>"}'
```

Two slots while the fee is 0: your signing address signs the agreement, and the payment address signs the funding (each slot names its `signer`; with one address, both are yours). An operator that charges a fee adds a third, `operatorFee.signature`, which the payment address signs too. Check each message exactly:

- `signature`, primary type `Agree`, under the Holdback domain: `{commitmentId: <id>, buyer: <you>, nonce: <agreeNonce>, expiresAt: <terms offerExpiresAt>}`.
- `fund.signature`, primary type `ReceiveWithAuthorization`, under the token's domain from your pinned deployment: `{from: <your payment address>, to: <the pinned collector>, value: <price + adjudication.fee>, validAfter: 0, validBefore: <terms fundBy>, nonce: <the funding nonce>}`. Only the escrow's collector can redeem it, and only for this commitment.
- `operatorFee.signature`, only when the fee is above 0: primary type `TransferWithAuthorization`, under the token's domain: `{from: <your payment address>, to: <fees.operator.to>, value: <the fee you computed>, validAfter: 0, validBefore: <terms fundBy>, nonce: <the operator fee nonce>}`. Refuse a higher `value`, another `to`, or a third slot when `fees.operator` is absent or gives 0.

**Pay from your own wallet.** The payment address can be a wallet your agent never holds, such as a person's own. Hand it each of the two messages as the file `eth_signTypedData_v4` takes: `{"types": {"EIP712Domain": [name, version, chainId, verifyingContract], "<primary type>": [...]}, "domain", "primaryType", "message"}`, with integers as decimal strings. Build each file from the typed data you rebuilt yourself, never from the prepare answer, and show one sentence with it: which address pays which address how much, until when, for which deal. Any wallet that signs typed data signs it; for a hardware wallet, Foundry's `cast wallet sign --data --from-file <file>` does (check the flags against your version). Before signing, the holder checks the file alone: it rebuilds the nonce from the terms it approved and its own pinned deployment (the rules contract, the escrow, the collector, the chain ID, the token's domain, the expiry margin), never from the file, and refuses any difference; the nonce is what binds the signature to Crow's rules contract and this deal. Take each signature back as 65 bytes of hex and recover it over your own typed data: it must recover to the payment address, or send nothing. A signature for another deal or another amount recovers to some other address. A contract wallet (ERC-1271) answers on chain only, so this offline check cannot pass it. `examples/buyer-signer.ts` (`fundingFiles`, then `agreeAndFund` with the signatures) and `examples/pay-from-wallet.ts` (`files`, `check`, `fund`) do all of this.

In `submit.body`, `fund` is `{type: "FundingAuthorization", scheme: "erc3009", commitmentId, from, value, token: <demo.token.id>, validAfter: "1970-01-01T00:00:00Z", validBefore: <fundBy>, nonce: <the funding nonce>, expiresAt: <fundBy>, signature}`, and `operatorFee` is `{type: "TransferAuthorization", scheme: "erc3009", purpose: "operator_fee", commitmentId, from, to, value, token, validAfter: "1970-01-01T00:00:00Z", validBefore: <fundBy>, nonce: <the operator fee nonce>, expiresAt: <fundBy>, signature}`: the same values as the typed data.

Submit the signatures in the one body to `/v1/commitments/<id>/agree`. The operator sends the funding, and any fee transfer, in one Sepolia transaction and answers once it is in a block (12 to 24 seconds). The answer reads `"escrowState": "Held"` with `hold.amount.amount` the price plus the adjudication fee: 26000000 for a 25 tUSDC price and a 1 tUSDC adjudication fee, which is all your balance drops by while the fee is 0. The events name the transaction (`ref/http.txt`, "Read the result"). An operator that charges a fee answers a body without it with 402 `payment_required`, with the amount in `fee`; these nodes charge none for now.

## Open the work

1. `GET /v1/commitments/<id>/key?recipient=<your address>` returns `{"items": [{"recipient", "scheme", "ciphertext"}], ...}`; empty until your key is released with payment. This envelope is not the one the seller sealed: on a judged deal the adjudicator re-seals the content key to the `buyerEncryptionPublicKey` in your terms and the settlement layer releases it with the payment, so it opens with your key either way. In the events, the first `key.released` after the delivery is usually the primary adjudicator's (released so it can judge); yours comes with the payment.
2. Decode the envelope: bytes 0 to 32 are the ephemeral public key, 32 to 56 the nonce, the rest the box. Open the box with your encryption secret key: that is the content key.
3. From the `delivery.submitted` event, take `payload.ciphertext.uri` and `payload.plaintextSha256`. `/events` returns `{"items": [...], "nextCursor"}`, newest first; each item has `seq`, `type` (the event's name, such as `delivery.submitted` or `key.released`), `commitmentId`, `at`, `actor` (who submitted it, when anyone did), `payload` and the hash-chain fields `prevHash` and `hash`. Fetch the ciphertext and check its SHA-256 against `payload.ciphertext.sha256`.
4. Decrypt: the first 12 bytes are the IV, the rest is AES-256-GCM ciphertext and tag. Check the plaintext's SHA-256 against `plaintextSha256`.
