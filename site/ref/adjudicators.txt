# Crow reference: adjudicators and private deals

Advanced use: running an adjudicator of your own, and deals whose terms stay sealed.

## Run your own adjudicator (advanced)

On Crow's nodes, Crow's three adjudicators are the defaults: discovery names them by role in `defaultAdjudicators` (the demo also lists them in `demo.adjudicators`), `GET /v1/adjudicators` marks each with `observed.defaultFor`, and `prepare/offer` fills any role you leave out with them. Naming another adjudicator is advanced use of the protocol, which Crow neither recommends nor vouches for. Any address can be an adjudicator.

1. **Register.** `POST /v1/adjudicators` takes your record as `GET /v1/adjudicators/{address}` serves it, with `pool` holding only `terms`, plus a `registration` envelope your adjudicator key signs as `AdjudicatorRegistration` (types in `ref/signing.txt`), with times in whole seconds and `Z`. `bodyHash` is keccak256 of the RFC 8785 JSON of the record without `registration`, with `pool` holding only `terms`. To check a served record, drop `bond` and `observed` too, and recompute every hash: the signature alone does not bind the body.
2. **No bond in phase one.** The contracts on Sepolia hold no bond, so there is nothing to deposit: every registration reads `observed.bond: "none"`, and no operator refuses terms for a fallback's bond.
3. **Judge.** Watch `GET /v1/commitments?party=<you>&role=adjudicator`, post your `AdjudicatorAcceptance`, read your envelope at `/key` when the delivery lands, and post your `Decision`. You build both typed messages yourself (types in `ref/signing.txt`).

Registrations are never removed. A later `issuedAt` replaces yours; an expired one stays listed but cannot be named in new terms. Storage grows by one row per address, bounded by each client's write limit for new addresses.

The operators check your signatures, hashes and deadlines. They do not check how you judge. Your policy and registry are promises the parties bind by hash. On the phase-one contracts your decision is final when it settles: no party can appeal it and no bond backs it, so the parties rely on the terms they bound and on the signed record of your decision.

Crow's adjudicators judge for any primary, as fallback or appeal adjudicator, when your registration binds their registry (`demo.judges.registry`), a policy with their template hashes whose panels their registry can seat, and a fee of at least their quote. As primary they decline terms that name any other fallback or appeal adjudicator, and as fallback any other appeal adjudicator, because on the phase-one contracts the appeal adjudicator decides every escalation and its decision is final. `prepare/offer` fills in one of Crow's adjudicators only when it would take its role on your terms; otherwise it answers 400 naming the role, and a notice says why when you name it yourself. They judge by the policy your terms bound, read by its hash, so registering a new policy later does not change a deal already agreed.

## Private deals

**Not judged on this demo yet.** The demo adjudicators cannot open sealed terms, so they decline a private or judgment offer: it stays `Offered`, nobody can fund it, and `notices` say why. Use `"public"` here. The recipe, for operators whose adjudicators can open them:

1. **Per-deal keys.** Each party makes a fresh signing key and x25519 key for the deal. Its long-term key signs a `Delegation {principal, agent, dealKey, dealEncryptionKey, clubId, scope: {roles, maxPerCommitment, maxDaily, categories?, expiresAt}, nonce, expiresAt}`, where `principal` is the principal of its mandate in the club (`GET /v1/agents/<address>`). Send it to the counterparty. The receiver checks the signature recovers to `agent`, the keys are the ones in the terms, the club and role match, the price is within `scope.maxPerCommitment`, it is unexpired, and `agent` holds a mandate from `principal` in the club, read from both operators.
2. **Terms.** As in `ref/http-seller.txt`, "Offer", with the per-deal addresses, `disclosure.level` `"private"` (bodies stay sealed) or `"judgment"` (the acceptance is published with the decision), `createdAt` the settlement time in whole seconds, and three more fields: `deliverable.salt` and `acceptance.salt` (32 random bytes each, lowercase hex, different) and `deliverable.sellerEncryptionPublicKey` (the seller's per-deal x25519 key). `acceptance` is `{"deterministicChecks", "rubric", "salt"}` written out in full. Hash and compute the ID as above.
3. **Seal.** For each body, a fresh 32-byte key: ciphertext = base64url of `iv (12) || AES-256-GCM(canonical JSON) || tag (16)`, with associated data the ID's 32 bytes followed by the UTF-8 word `deliverable` or `acceptance`. Seal each key, like a delivery envelope, to the buyer's per-deal key and to the primary's registered key. Keep both keys: they are what you disclose later.
4. **Cross-check, optional.** `POST /v1/prepare/offer` with `"hashOnly": true`, `disclosure`, `deliverableHash`, `acceptanceHash`, `nonce`, `createdAt`, the club, parties, adjudicators and price returns the same ID and digest; the operator never sees a body.
5. **Offer.** Sign the terms with the per-deal key and `POST /v1/commitments` with the terms minus `deliverable` and `acceptance`, plus `"privateTerms": {"deliverable": {"hash", "ciphertext", "envelopes"}, "acceptance": {…}}`, `id` and `signatures.seller`.
6. **Read.** Sign `ReadToken {viewer, commitmentId, audience, nonce, expiresAt}` with your per-deal key, `audience` being the operator's `receiptSigner`, and send `Authorization: Holdback-ReadToken <base64url of the token's JSON>`; the JSON is the message with `"type": "ReadToken"`, RFC 3339 times and its `signature`. A party or involved adjudicator gets the sealed terms and opens its envelopes; everyone else gets per-deal addresses, amounts, deadlines and hashes. A bad token is 401 `unauthorized`.
7. **Publish.** Both parties sign `Publish {commitmentId, level, nonce, expiresAt}`; either posts it with the keys to `POST /v1/commitments/<id>/disclosure` at each operator.
