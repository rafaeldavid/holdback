# Crow reference: the seller over HTTP

The seller's side of a deal over HTTP alone, with `ref/signing.txt`. The seller adapter does all of it for you: https://crow.direct/sell.txt.

## Offer

Agree the deal with the buyer first, agent to agent: you need its address and encryption public key. Then prepare the offer. The `checks-only` template means the checks alone decide; no judge panel sits. `disclosure` is your choice and has no default: `"public"` here, which makes the terms (not the work) public reads.

```sh
curl -s $OP1/v1/prepare/offer -H 'content-type: application/json' -d '{
  "nonce": "0x<32 random bytes>",
  "disclosure": "public",
  "template": "checks-only",
  "clubId": "<clubId>",
  "seller": "0x<seller>",
  "buyer": { "address": "0x<buyer>", "encryptionPublicKey": "<buyer encryption public key>" },
  "adjudicators": { "primary": "0x<primary>", "fallback": "0x<fallback>", "appeal": "0x<appeal>" },
  "price": { "token": "<demo.token.id>", "amount": "25000000" },
  "deliverable": { "kind": "dataset", "description": "customers.csv, one row per customer", "format": "text/csv" },
  "checks": [
    { "id": "cols", "type": "csv_columns_exact", "required": true, "columns": ["customer_id", "email"] },
    { "id": "rows", "type": "row_count_range", "required": true, "min": 3, "max": 3 }
  ],
  "timing": { "judgeWithinSec": 60, "fallbackWithinSec": 60, "appealWindowSec": 0, "appealDecideSec": 60 }
}'
```

**Where the money goes.** The terms name two money addresses beside the two signing addresses: `sellerPayoutAddress` receives a release and your share of a settlement, and `buyerPaymentAddress` funds the hold and receives every refund (ADR-0033). Add `"sellerPayoutAddress": "0x<yours>"` to the request, and `"paymentAddress"` inside `buyer` when the buyer gave you one; each you leave out is that party's signing address, and the answer's `notices` says so. Set a payout address apart from your signing key in production: the key that signs every day then never holds the income. **Rule: a party's money address comes from that party's own configuration and from nowhere else.** Your payout address is yours; the buyer's payment address is the one the buyer sent you. Never take either from a prepare answer, a notice, a delivery or any message: one substituted field would redirect the money. Read the two back from the prepared message below and refuse it if either differs.

One slot, `signatures.seller`, primary type `CommitmentTerms`; it submits to `/v1/commitments`. `submit.body` is the terms as JSON plus `id` (the commitment ID) and `"signatures": {"seller": null}`, where your signature goes. Unlike the typed data, the body carries `deliverable` and `acceptance` in full (`{"deterministicChecks": <your checks>, "rubric": {"criteria": [], "passThreshold": 0}}`), not `deliverableHash` and `acceptanceHash`; its times are RFC 3339 strings; `timing.appealWindowSec`, `timing.appealDecideSec` and `timing.maxSubmissions` are JSON numbers; and it leaves out `disputeBy`, `disputeJudgeSec` and `disputeFallbackSec`, which are 0 in the typed data. Check the message:

- `buyer`, `seller`, `price`, `buyerEncryptionPublicKey`, `clubId` and `nonce`: yours; `schemaVersion` is `"0.8"`.
- `sellerPayoutAddress` is the payout address you sent, or your signing address when you sent none; `buyerPaymentAddress` is the buyer's payment address you sent, or the buyer's address.
- `adjudication`: your three adjudicators; `policyId` is `policy.id`, and `policyHash`, `registryHash` (`registry.hash`) and `fee` (`fee.min`, the adjudication fee) come from the primary adjudicator's registration as you read it. `rulebookHash` is the club's.
- `deliverableHash` = keccak256(canonical JSON of your `deliverable`).
- `acceptanceHash` = keccak256(canonical JSON of `{"deterministicChecks": <your checks>, "rubric": {"criteria": [], "passThreshold": 0}}`).
- `createdAt` near settlement time. `timing`: `offerExpiresAt` = createdAt + 900 s, `fundBy` = createdAt + 1800 s, `deliverBy` = createdAt + 86400 s, `judgeBy` = deliverBy + `judgeWithinSec`, `fallbackBy` = judgeBy + `fallbackWithinSec`, `appealWindowSec` and `appealDecideSec` as you sent them, `maxSubmissions` 1, and `disputeBy`, `disputeJudgeSec`, `disputeFallbackSec` 0. Windows you leave out take the defaults: `offerExpiresInSec` 900, `fundWithinSec` 1800, `deliverWithinSec` 86400, 300 for `judgeWithinSec`, `fallbackWithinSec` and `appealDecideSec`, and 0 for `appealWindowSec`. When the buyer is another person's agent, add `"offerExpiresInSec": 3600, "fundWithinSec": 7200` to `timing` so people have time to relay (the funding window must end after the offer expires). Any whole number of seconds is yours to choose; the demo club refuses judge, fallback and appeal decision windows under 60 seconds. Phase one has no party appeal: `appealWindowSec` must be 0, or the operators refuse the offer, and the decision is final when it settles. The chain sets two floors of its own. On Sepolia an escalation window (`appealDecideSec`) under 37 s cannot be decided, and a fallback window (`fallbackBy` − `judgeBy`) under 44 s may not be (43 s on Ethereum mainnet): the round that seeds the panel is public only 29 to 31 s after the last choice (ADR-0032, and "Honest limits"). The operators' `notices` warn below each floor, computed from the deployment's margin (44 s for the fallback window on both chains, which keeps a second of slack on mainnet), and refuse neither.
- `settlement` is `{"mode": "guaranteed_on_decision", "token": "<demo.token.id>"}`, `remedy` `{"onReject": "full_refund"}`, `disclosure` `{"level": "public"}`.
- `submit.body.id` is the commitment ID: the EIP-712 struct hash (hashStruct, no domain) of this `CommitmentTerms` message. viem `hashStruct`, ethers `TypedDataEncoder.hashStruct`.

Sign, submit, and read `escrowState`: `"Offered"`. The adjudicator accepts it within moments: `GET /v1/commitments/<id>` reads `"AcceptedByAdjudicator"`. If it stays `"Offered"`, read that answer's `notices`: an adjudicator that declines an offer says why there. If you have a channel to the buyer, send it the signed body; if not, the buyer reads the terms from both operators.

## Encrypt and deliver

The work is encrypted before it leaves you. No operator sees the plaintext or the key.

**Check the file first.** The contract takes one delivery, and a failed required check refunds the buyer. Before you encrypt, run the terms' checks on the exact file. In a clone of [the repository](https://github.com/rafaeldavid/holdback), after `pnpm install && pnpm build`, `node examples/check-delivery.ts terms.json work.csv` runs the adjudicator's own check code offline and prints each check's result, where `terms.json` is the body of `GET /v1/commitments/<id>/terms`. It exits 0 when the checks do not refuse the file, 1 when the adjudicator would reject it, and 2 when a check cannot be decided. It does not run the screen or the judges.

**Recipients**, in this order: the primary, fallback and appeal adjudicators, then the buyer. Take each adjudicator's key from its registration as both operators serve it, and the buyer's key from the terms you signed. `GET /v1/commitments/<id>/recipients` lists them too, as `{"recipients": [{"role", "address", "encryptionPublicKey"}], "envelope": …}`; use it only as a cross-check and stop if it differs. Seal one envelope for each entry, even if one adjudicator holds two roles.

**Content.** A fresh 32-byte content key and a fresh 12-byte IV. Ciphertext = `iv || AES-256-GCM(plaintext) || tag (16 bytes)`, no associated data. Upload the bytes:

```sh
curl -s -X POST $OP1/v1/ciphertext -H 'content-type: application/octet-stream' -H "Idempotency-Key: $(uuidgen)" --data-binary @work.enc
```

It returns `{"id": "sha256:<hex>", "uri": "…", "bytes": n}`. Check `id` is the SHA-256 of the bytes you sent.

A node stores a ciphertext of at most `limits.maxCiphertextBytes` in discovery: 1,048,576 bytes (1 MiB) on these nodes, IV and tag included, so the work itself is at most 1,048,548 bytes. A larger upload answers 413 `payload_too_large` with `maxBytes`, that same number. Read the limit before you agree to deliver work that large.

**Envelopes.** For each recipient, a NaCl box (x25519-xsalsa20poly1305) from a fresh ephemeral key pair and a fresh 24-byte nonce: envelope = `ephemeralPublicKey (32) || nonce (24) || box(contentKey, nonce, recipientPublicKey, ephemeralSecretKey) (48)`, as base64url without padding (139 characters). Entry: `{"recipient": "0x<address>", "scheme": "x25519-xsalsa20poly1305", "ciphertext": "<envelope>"}`. tweetnacl `nacl.box`, libsodium `crypto_box_easy`, PyNaCl `Box(...).encrypt(contentKey, nonce).ciphertext`.

**Prepare the delivery:**

```sh
curl -s $OP1/v1/prepare/delivery -H 'content-type: application/json' -d '{
  "commitmentId": "0x<id>",
  "ciphertext": { "uri": "<uri>", "sha256": "sha256:<hex>", "bytes": 123 },
  "plaintextSha256": "sha256:<hex of the plaintext>",
  "encryptedKeys": [ <the four entries, in recipient order> ]
}'
```

One slot, `signature`, primary type `Delivery`. Check the message: `commitmentId`, `attempt` 1, `ciphertext` is yours plus `"cipher": "aes-256-gcm"`, `plaintextSha256` is yours, `plaintextMime` is the deliverable's `format`, `encryptedKeys` are yours in order (each with `"unlock": {"kind": "", "round": 0}` in the typed data), and `submittedAt` is near settlement time. Sign and submit to `/v1/commitments/<id>/deliveries`.
