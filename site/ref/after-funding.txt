# Crow reference: after funding

What either party can do once a deal is funded, besides waiting for the adjudicator.

## Beyond the happy path

Each is prepared, verified and submitted as `ref/signing.txt`, "Prepare, verify, sign, submit", says.

- **Early accept (buyer, only in `Delivered`).** `POST /v1/prepare/early-accept {"commitmentId", "nonce"}`; one slot, `EarlyAccept {commitmentId, buyer, attempt, deliveryHash, nonce, expiresAt}`; submit to `/v1/commitments/<id>/accept`. The seller is paid now, your key is released to you, the adjudication fee comes back to you, and the deal is final. So before you sign, open your own envelope: get the seller's signed delivery from the seller, check its delivery hash equals `payload.deliveryHash` of `delivery.submitted` at both operators, open your envelope, decrypt, and check `plaintextSha256`. Then check `deliveryHash` in the message is that hash: put the hash of the delivery you opened there, and the contract refuses the accept relayed with any other delivery. If anything fails, wait for the adjudicator instead.
- **Settlement by agreement (both parties, in `Held`, `Delivered`, `FallbackJudging` or `Escalated`).** `POST /v1/prepare/settlement {"commitmentId", "nonce", "toSellerBps"}` (or `payouts: [{"to", "amount": {"token", "amount"}}]` summing to the price, each `to` the buyer's payment address or the seller's payout address, the only two the phase-one contracts pay; optional `releaseKey`, `expiresAt`). Both sign the one `MutualSettlement {commitmentId, hasDelivery, deliveryHash, payouts, releaseKey, nonce, expiresAt}` (slots `buyerSignature` and `sellerSignature`); either submits to `/v1/commitments/<id>/settlements`. The adjudication fee returns to the buyer.
- **Settling late work (both parties, in `Held`).** When no delivery is posted, for example because the work came after `deliverBy`, the settlement can name the delivery the buyer holds: send the seller's signed delivery as `delivery` in the prepare call, and the settlement releases its key to the buyer. `releaseKey` needs a delivery, and the delivery needs an envelope for the buyer. The operators' reads do not show a late delivery's hash, so keep the signed delivery and the settlement as your record.
- **Appeal: not in phase one.** The contracts on Sepolia take no party appeal: `POST /v1/prepare/appeal` and `POST /v1/commitments/<id>/appeals` answer 409 `invalid_state` with the reason, and the first decision by the primary or the fallback adjudicator is final. The full protocol's appeal, with its bond, runs on SimChain only (`pnpm demo`).
- **Submitting directly to the contract.** These operators read the contract's logs and show your step within a minute, with `submittedVia: "direct"`. The contract keeps only a delivery's hash, so after a direct `deliver` also POST the same signed delivery to `/v1/commitments/{id}/deliveries`, at once or later. The operator joins it with no second delivery on chain and hands the adjudicators their keys; without that post they cannot open the work. If the operator cannot read your transaction yet, it answers 409 `invalid_state` and says it keeps your copy. It joins the copy within about a minute, and a retry with the same `Idempotency-Key` then answers `Delivered`.
- **Deadlines close deals on their own.** Once a deadline passes, the operators send its timeout at their own gas. They wait a minute, so you can send it first, and send it at their next pass: one to two minutes after the deadline in all. The contract checks each before they send it. They send:
  - `reclaim`, after `deliverBy` with nothing delivered: the buyer gets price and fee back;
  - the default, after `fallbackBy` with no fallback decision, or after `appealDecideBy` for an escalation: the buyer gets price and fee back.

  An offer nobody funded expires at `offerExpiresAt` by the operators' clock: their own record, with no transaction, and from then the contract refuses to fund it. They do not send the fallback takeover before `fallbackBy`, which is the fallback adjudicator's turn, and they cap how many they send a day. An early accept or a settlement by agreement after a deadline has only those one to two minutes: sign it before.
- **Your own trigger (anyone, no signature).** You need not wait for the operators: `POST /v1/commitments/<id>/triggers/<kind>` with `{}` once the deadline has passed (`reclaim`, `fallback`, `default`, `apply-default`). On the phase-one contracts `default` refunds price and fee in one call, so `apply-default` then has nothing to send; `appeal-expiry` is not in phase one and answers 409 `invalid_state`. Too early is 409 `invalid_state`. Once this node has recorded the timeout, whoever sent it, the call answers 200 with the state it left and sends nothing. A deal behind the chain (below), or a timeout you sent straight to the contract before the operators read it (within about a minute), still gets the contract's 409.
