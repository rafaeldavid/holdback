How Crow handles money, keys and data

Where the money sits

When the buyer funds a deal, the payment goes straight into an escrow contract on Ethereum Sepolia, a public test network.3, 4 The escrow is Coinbase’s Commerce Payments escrow, used without changes.5 The payment never passes through an account that Crow controls. Inside the escrow, the tokens sit in a token store that the escrow creates for Crow’s rules contract. Etherscan shows the balance there, not at the escrow’s own address.6

The payment hold opens in only five ways, all set by the deal’s terms:7, 8, 9

  • the reviewer signs a decision: accept pays the seller, and reject refunds the buyer;
  • the buyer signs an early accept;
  • both parties sign a settlement;
  • the seller misses its delivery deadline, and the buyer gets a refund;
  • no reviewer decides in time, and the default outcome applies, which on the live service refunds the buyer.

As a last backstop, if money is still held 24 hours after the deal’s last decision deadline, the buyer’s payment address can take it back directly from the escrow.10

Anyone can trigger the late-delivery refund or the default outcome once it is due. Crow’s operators do it on their own and pay the gas themselves.11, 12

The price only ever goes to the seller’s payout address or back to the buyer’s payment address. If the terms set a reviewer’s fee, the reviewer that decides is paid that fee from the hold when it decides.11, 13

The money is a test token called tUSDC. Anyone can mint it, it is worth nothing, and it is not Circle’s USDC. No real money can enter Crow today.14, 15, 12

Who can move the money, and who cannot

No one at Crow can move a held payment outside the deal’s terms. The contracts have no admin key, no pause switch and no way to upgrade them, and the key that deployed them only paid the gas.14, 16

Crow’s operators pass the parties’ signed messages to the contracts and pay the gas. Their keys have no role in any contract. The rules contract checks every signature itself, so a message that was changed on the way fails.11

Crow runs the reviewers on the live service, and a reviewer’s signed decision does move the payment: to the seller, or back to the buyer, as the terms say. The buyer and the seller name all three reviewers in the terms they sign, and each decision is final once it settles.13, 17, 12

If Crow’s operators refuse a deal or go offline, either party can send the same signed messages straight to the contracts, using its own Sepolia ETH. Today the two operators share one process, so most failures hit both.18, 12 Crow’s reviewers run in the same service. While it is down, a deal that named them ends at its deadlines with the default refund, unless the buyer accepts early or both parties sign a settlement.19, 18

With a real stablecoin, the issuer could freeze its own token. No one at Crow can. Each deployment works with the one token it was deployed with.17

What Crow holds, and what it does not

Crow holds none of your company’s keys. Your agents sign with keys they keep, and the buyer’s tool creates each deal’s keys on your machine and never sends them anywhere.20, 21

On the test service, Crow’s demo principal signs every agent’s mandate, the standing approval of what the agent may sign. So on the test service, the authority that stands for your company is a Crow key. When a company runs its own club, it signs its agents’ mandates itself.22, 23

Crow’s operators never write a message on a party’s behalf. Every step that changes a deal carries that party’s own signature, and anyone can check it.11

Crow keeps its own secrets as Cloudflare Worker secrets, never in the code repository: the operators’ gas key on Sepolia, the model providers’ keys for the reviewers’ judges, and the test seller’s seed.24 The reviewers’ and the operators’ signing keys, which sign the decisions that move payments, work differently: they come from a random secret that the operators’ Durable Object keeps in its own storage.25

The seller encrypts the work before sending it, for the first reviewer and the buyer. A backup reviewer gets the key only if it takes over. The reviewer opens the work to judge it, and the buyer opens it after paying. Crow runs the reviewers on the live service, so Crow can read the work in the deals it reviews.26, 17

What a deal makes public

Every deal on the live service is public today. Anyone can read its terms: the scope, the checks, the price, the deadlines, the reviewers and every address.27

The work itself stays encrypted. Even so, anyone can see its exact size, when it arrived, and a fingerprint that confirms a guessed copy.26

If the terms include a rubric, two model providers read the work, and the judges’ public notes quote its findings. A deal judged by checks alone sends the work to no model provider.28

The chain shows the amounts and the time of each step, and links each payment and payout address to the deal’s signing key. Use a fresh key and fresh addresses for each deal.28

What the operators keep, and for how long

The operators keep a request log with one line per request: its ID, the time, the node, the method, the route, the status and how long it took. The log never holds a request body, a header, an IP address or a path. Each line is deleted after 7 days.29, 30

They also write hourly and daily summaries (counts, timings and the deal funnel, with no addresses in them) and error lines to Cloudflare’s Workers Logs, which keeps them for 7 days. Request headers are not logged.29

The operators see each caller’s IP address while they pass on its request, but they do not write it to the request log.29, 31

The live operators do not accept problem reports yet. Their report route, POST /v1/feedback, is switched off, so they keep no reports.32

Deal records (the terms, the events and the receipts) stay as long as the contracts they settle on are in use, and start over only when the contracts are deployed again. The chain keeps its part for good, so keep your own copy of the terms you sign.12

The early-access sign-up keeps your email address, the time and the page you signed up from. It keeps no IP address and sets no cookie, and it has no deletion date yet.33

What has been reviewed from outside, and what has not

Coinbase’s escrow contract, which holds the money, has had outside reviews. Cantina reviewed the whole contract in April 2025 and found one critical issue, which was fixed before release. In July 2026, Cantina reviewed its last code change, PR 90, at the exact commit Crow deploys, and found three informational issues. One later change, the renames in PR 71, has not been reviewed.34, 35, 5

Crow’s own rules contract, which decides when the escrow pays out, has not had an outside audit yet. Models from two AI families have reviewed it, and the scope an auditor would need is written up.14, 2

Crow also runs its own checks: unit, fuzz and invariant tests of the contracts, a TLA+ model of a deal’s states, and attack and neutrality scenarios for the judging and the operators.36, 37, 38

Each contract’s source code is published on Sourcify. The deployment record names the commit and the compiler settings, so anyone can rebuild the bytecode and compare it.14, 3

Not done yet: a penetration test, SOC 2, ISO 27001 or any other certification, and a bug bounty. A bug bounty sized to the funds at risk will start before any deployment with real money.39

How to report a problem

If you find a security problem, report it privately through GitHub’s private vulnerability reporting for Crow’s repository. Crow acknowledges each report within 3 business days and will not take legal action over research that follows the policy.40, 39, 41

For any other problem with a call or a guide, open an issue on Crow’s public repository. Issues are public, so leave out keys and anything you need to keep to yourself. The operators’ own report route is not switched on yet.42, 32

To see whether Crow is answering right now, open the status page. It checks each part when you open it.43

Each answer was checked against its source on 2026-11-14. The addresses and the retention periods come from the deployment record and the code each time the site is built.
